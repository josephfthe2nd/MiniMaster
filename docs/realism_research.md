# Realistic human — research findings

Compiled from an 8-angle research sweep (anatomy, character-creator internals,
Blender modeling practice, topology standards, realism cues, print limits).


## body-anatomy — canonical proportion systems, measured landmarks, male/female skeletal dimorphism, and muscle-mass placement, audited directly against MiniMaster's current cage numbers

### Findings

- **The current MiniMaster figure is 6.77 heads tall — noticeably shorter than any realistic canon.**
  - Measured from the live code (/home/user/MiniMaster/minimaster/character/generator.py): HEAD_UNIT = 0.138 H, chin base 0.80, sole z = 0.004, so figure height = 0.934 and height/head = 6.77 (head = 14.8% of height). Realistic canon is 7.5 heads (head = 13.3%), Loomis 'ideal' is 8 heads (12.5%), heroic 8.5. Loomis explicitly recommends the 8-head ideal over the real-life 7.5-head average for illustration.
  - *Implication:* Every other proportion in the model is expressed in fractions of H, so the whole body currently reads 'stylized/chibi-heroic', not 'actual realistic'. This is the single largest realism defect and it is a one-constant fix, plus it should become a slider (a `head_units` / `proportion` control from 6.5 to 8.5) rather than a hard-coded constant.
  - Source: https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/human-proportions-idealistic-figures/
- **Loomis's 8-head landmark ladder gives exact ring heights the 9-ring torso can be snapped to.**
  - Crown 0; chin 1; nipples 2; navel 3; crotch 4 (exact mid-point of the figure); knee 6; sole 8. Shoulders 2⅓ heads wide, hips 1.5 heads wide, nipple-to-nipple 1 head. Cross-checks used by every atelier: elbow at the navel/natural waist; wrist at the greater trochanter / crotch line; fingertips at mid-thigh; arm span = total height; foot length ≈ one head ≈ forearm length; hand length ≈ face length (chin-to-hairline) ≈ ¾ head.
  - *Implication:* Gives a direct target table for the R0–R8 torso ring z-values and for the arm/leg joint layout. The current model already satisfies elbow-at-waist and wrist-at-crotch (measured elbow frac 0.660 vs waist ring 0.660; wrist frac 0.531 vs crotch 0.515) — but violates the head-count, the hand size and the nipple line.
  - Source: https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Page:Andrew_Loomis,_Figure_Drawing_for_All_It's_Worth.pdf/19
- **The model's male shoulders are ~30% wider than real anthropometry; the female shoulders are roughly correct.**
  - Measured from the code: deltoid-to-deltoid width = 0.346 H (male), 0.309 H (neutral), 0.273 H (female). Real bideltoid shoulder breadth for US men averages ~46.7 cm against ~172.7–175 cm stature ≈ 0.266–0.270 of height. Skeletal biacromial breadth as % of stature was 23.01 (male) and 22.57 (female) in the 2015 7th Korean Anthropometric Survey — i.e. skeletal shoulder breadth relative to height is almost identical between sexes.
  - *Implication:* The `gender` composite currently multiplies shoulders by (1 − 0.14·gm), so male gets +14% and female −14%, on top of an already-heroic base. Real dimorphism in shoulder BREADTH is ~2%; the perceived male width comes from deltoid/trap MUSCLE volume, not skeleton. Fix: shrink the skeletal shoulder coupling to ±3% and move the remaining difference into the deltoid radius and trapezius mass.
  - Source: https://fitnessvolt.com/average-shoulder-width/
- **The female preset is over-feminized: shoulders end up NARROWER than hips, which is not what real skeletons do.**
  - Measured ratios in the code: shoulder ring R6 / hip ring R1 = 1.49 (male), 1.13 (neutral), 0.86 (female). Deltoid/hip = 1.82 / 1.42 / 1.10. Published biacromial:biiliac ('androgyny index') is ≈1.4 for males and ≈1.2 for females in US adults; Hughes & Gallup report shoulder-to-hip 1.18 male / 1.03 female using a different (circumference-style) protocol. In every dataset the female value stays above 1.0.
  - *Implication:* The female silhouette should be produced by a NARROW WAIST plus a wide TROCHANTER, not by narrow shoulders. Retarget female R6/R1 to ~1.20 and male to ~1.40, and put the difference into waist and trochanter instead.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_proportions
- **The widest point of the hip silhouette is at the greater trochanter, which is level with the pubic symphysis (the crotch) — the model puts its widest hip ring above the crotch, at iliac-crest level.**
  - Anatomically, the greater trochanter is the lateral bony prominence of the femur and sits at roughly the same height as the pubic symphysis; the iliac crest sits about a head higher and is NARROWER in soft-tissue silhouette. In the code R0 (crotch, z-frac 0.515) has fixed width 0.192 H and is not driven by `hips` at all, while R1 (z-frac 0.563) is the slider-driven widest ring (0.231 H at female). Female pelvic outlet is larger, so inter-trochanteric distance is greater in women.
  - *Implication:* The current cage produces a 'saddlebag above the crotch' read and a straight-sided pelvis. Splitting the pelvic block into a trochanter ring (at/just above crotch, widest, driven by `hips` and `glute_drive`) and an iliac-crest ring (higher, narrower, driven by `hips` at ~0.85×) is the highest-yield single topology change for the lower body.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_trochanter
- **The torso ring stack sits 4–6% of height too high, compressing the abdomen.**
  - Measured z-fractions in the code: crotch 0.515, hip 0.563, waist 0.660, ribs 0.702, chest/nipple 0.767, shoulder 0.842. Canon/anthropometry targets: crotch = 0.50 (exact half), navel ≈ 0.60–0.62, natural waist (narrowest) ≈ 0.62–0.63, bottom of ribcage / 10th rib ≈ 0.665–0.68, nipple line ≈ 0.72–0.735 (2 heads down of 7.5–8), acromion ≈ 0.81–0.82.
  - *Implication:* Fixing the head-unit alone will not fix this — the ring z's are authored as absolute fractions of H. Every torso ring needs re-anchoring, and the gap between the ribs ring and the hip ring (currently only 0.702−0.563 = 0.139 H) needs to widen to ~0.10–0.12 H of genuinely soft abdomen with its own ring.
  - Source: https://www.thedrawingsource.com/figure-drawing-proportions.html
- **Hands are ~30% too small and feet are ~17% too long relative to real anthropometry.**
  - Measured from code: hand length (wrist to fingertip) = 0.077 H = 0.52 heads; foot length = 0.175 H = 1.19 heads. Real: mean hand length 19.3 cm (male) / 17.2 cm (female) against mean stature 172.7 / 157.2 cm → hand ≈ 0.109–0.112 of stature (0.82–0.84 heads at 7.5). Artistic canon: hand = face length ≈ 0.75 head. Reported mean foot length 24.7 cm ≈ 0.15 of stature ≈ 1.1 heads. Canon also says foot length ≈ forearm length; the model's forearm is 0.120 vs foot 0.164 — a 37% mismatch.
  - *Implication:* Undersized mitten hands are one of the strongest 'not realistic' tells at any viewing distance, and they get worse when the head shrinks to 7.5 units. Target hand length 0.108 H and foot length 0.152 H, and enforce foot ≈ forearm as a constraint.
  - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S209050681830215X
- **Limb-segment ratios in the model are already anatomically correct and should be left alone.**
  - Measured: forearm/upper-arm = 0.75 (brachial index; human range ~0.74–0.80). Tibia/femur = 0.85 (crural index; human range ~0.80–0.86). Leg length crotch-to-floor = 0.515 of height (canon 0.50). Knee joint at z-frac 0.274 (tibiale height in anthropometry ≈ 0.28). Calf ring L3 at z 0.205 sits almost exactly on the classic construction rule for the widest calf point.
  - *Implication:* Do not touch these. They are the parts of the current cage that already read correctly, and effort should go elsewhere. Worth adding a regression test that pins brachial index to 0.74–0.80 and crural index to 0.80–0.86 across all slider extremes.
  - Source: https://www.artistsnetwork.com/art-mediums/drawing/beginner-drawing-understanding-anatomy-drawing-the-leg/
- **The limb silhouette is governed by four asymmetry rules that a symmetric tube cage cannot express.**
  - (1) Widest calf point = the midpoint between the knee and the knee-ankle midpoint, i.e. ~25% down the shank. (2) The calf mass is HIGHER on the outside (lateral gastroc) and LOWER on the inside (medial gastroc). (3) The ankle reverses this: the medial malleolus is HIGHER than the lateral. (4) The quads form an inverted heart just above the knee, with vastus lateralis riding higher than vastus medialis; the forearm is thickest just below the elbow (~25–30% down) and tapers to a bony wrist. The deltoid runs to roughly the middle of the humerus, inserting at the deltoid tuberosity just above the humeral midpoint, with the arm as a whole tapering shoulder→wrist in an undulating swell-and-narrow rhythm.
  - *Implication:* These are per-column (azimuthal) offsets, not per-ring radii. The 8-column leg/arm tubes have exactly the columns needed (medial/lateral/anterior/posterior at ±22.5°, ±67.5°) — the missing piece is a per-column z-offset table so the medial and lateral bellies can be staggered vertically instead of sharing a ring plane.
  - Source: https://www.idrawfashion.com/body/body-parts/24-how-to-draw-legs/
- **Female skeletal dimorphism is concentrated in the pelvis, the ribcage taper, and the joint angles — not in overall bone gracility of the shoulder girdle.**
  - The female pelvis is lower (not as tall) and proportionately wider; the sacrum is wider, the pelvic ring wider and more circular, so acetabula are farther apart and greater-trochanter separation increases. Shoulder skeletal breadth is proportionately the same as male — larger male muscular development makes it LOOK wider. The female thorax is more rounded and smaller, and more constricted at its base, which combined with a lower pelvis produces the longer, narrower waist. Wider hips angle the femora inward, giving a slight X (valgus) leg. Carrying angle of the elbow: 10.97° ± 4.27 male vs 15.07° ± 4.95 female (one study; commonly quoted ranges 5–10° male, 10–15° female). Knee Q-angle: ~12–13.5° male vs ~15.9–17° female. Lumbar lordosis is greater in females (27.6° ± 8.0 vs 23.7° ± 6.1 in an adolescent cohort); one adult cohort found standing pelvic tilt 7.8° female vs 14.3° male (note: sign convention differs across papers — treat magnitude, not direction, as settled).
  - *Implication:* Gives concrete numeric couplings for the `gender` composite: pelvis height ×0.93 female, pelvic width ×1.08, ribcage base taper +8% constriction, elbow carrying angle 8°→15°, knee valgus 13°→16°, and a ~4° extra lumbar curve. The model currently has none of the angular dimorphism — arms and legs are straight columns, which is a strong 'mannequin' tell.
  - Source: https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/see-the-skeletal-differences-between-women-and-men
- **Fat deposits on a directional, sex-specific map (android vs gynoid), so a single scalar `weight` slider that inflates everything uniformly is wrong.**
  - Android (male-pattern) fat goes to neck, chest, shoulders and abdomen — above and around the waist. Gynoid (female-pattern) fat goes to breasts, hips, thighs and buttocks. Testosterone drives abdominal/gluteofemoral deposition patterns, oestrogen drives thigh/hip/buttock. Research groups women with WHR ≥ 0.85 as android and < 0.78 as gynoid. ACE body-fat bands: men essential 2–5%, athletic 6–13%, fitness 14–17%, acceptable 18–24%, obese 25%+; women essential 10–13%, athletic 14–20%, fitness 21–24%, acceptable 25–31%, obese 32%+ — every band sits ~8–10 points higher for women.
  - *Implication:* The weight slider should route mass through a gender-blended deposition mask, and should be re-labelled as body-fat %, mapping the slider range onto the ACE bands so the extremes are meaningful rather than arbitrary. The current code applies a nearly uniform ws = 1 + 0.30·w with a fixed front/back split and a small belly offset — no thigh/glute channel, no upper-arm channel, no submental/neck channel.
  - Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_fat_distribution
- **Breast placement has hard anatomical anchors the `bust` slider is currently ignoring.**
  - The breast spans roughly the 2nd to 6th ribs (extending lower as it matures/ptoses), horizontally from the sternal edge to the midaxillary line; the nipple sits at the level of the 4th rib / 4th intercostal space, just LATERAL to the midclavicular line, superior to the inframammary crease. In the code, bust displacement is applied only to columns 10–13 of ring R4 (z-frac 0.767) as a pure −y push with a small −z, and both the apex height and the lateral position are fixed regardless of size.
  - *Implication:* Real breasts translate laterally and drop as they grow, and the inframammary crease is a separate silhouette event. A three-ring bust (upper pole at rib 2 level, apex at rib 4, crease at rib 6) with size-coupled lateral splay and apex drop would take the female torso from 'bumps' to 'anatomy' with no topology change to the arms or legs.
  - Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519575/
- **The industry-standard answer to 'deep customization without slider explosion' is a learned PCA shape basis, not more hand-authored sliders.**
  - SMPL learns male and female linear body-shape models from the CAESAR dataset (~2000 scans per gender) via PCA; typically 10 principal components ('betas') suffice to explain most population variance in height, weight, muscularity and proportion, and these become blend shapes layered on top of linear blend skinning — exactly the pipeline MiniMaster already has. MakeHuman takes the alternative route: piecewise multi-linear interpolation between prototypical corner meshes (e.g. female/young/min-muscle/max-weight) for its macro sliders gender, age, muscle, weight, height, proportions, with per-region detail modifiers layered on top.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's `gender` composite is already a hand-tuned re-weighting of ~16 couplings — i.e. a hand-authored principal component. Formalizing this as an explicit basis of named 'macro vectors' (each a full vector of per-ring/per-column deltas) with the detail sliders layered additively on top makes the system extensible to 100+ effective controls without the coupling code growing quadratically. It also gives an obvious path to a 'randomize a plausible human' button by sampling the basis with realistic covariance.
  - Source: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/287815055_SMPL_a_skinned_multi-person_linear_model
- **Realistic subdivision cages route edge loops along muscle direction, and the current 9-ring torso is roughly half the loop density the anatomy needs.**
  - Practitioner guidance: edge loops should wrap over the deltoid cap and connect to pecs/clavicle in front and scapula behind, with sliding loops for clavicle lift and scapular glide; torso loops run along the obliques and lats with a calm centreline over the spine; bicep loops flow shoulder→elbow along the muscle. For cylinders, use vertex counts in steps of 4 (8, 12, 16, 20, 24) so cap loops stay central; 8 or 12 is the common low-poly arm/leg count and 8–10 faces around the body at the crotch merge point is the usual base-mesh figure. Loops built along muscle direction give control over contour and silhouette in any pose.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's 16-column torso and 8-column limbs are already on the right modulus. What is missing is RINGS: 9 torso rings cannot express clavicle, trochanter, navel and inframammary crease simultaneously. Going to 13–14 torso rings at 16 columns costs ~80 extra cage verts (403 → ~485) and no change to the subdivision or skinning machinery.
  - Source: https://novedge.com/blogs/design-news/zbrush-tip-anatomy-driven-retopology-for-predictable-deformation

### Recommendations

- [small/transformative] **Make head-height-count a first-class slider instead of a hard-coded constant, and move the default toward realism.** — In generator.py, HEAD_UNIT = 0.138 currently yields 6.77 heads (figure height 0.934). Solving Hh = k·(0.796 + Hh) for chin base 0.80 and sole 0.004: 7.5 heads → HEAD_UNIT = 0.1224; 8.0 heads → 0.1137; 8.5 heads → 0.1063. Add slider `proportion_heads` with range 6.5–8.5, default 7.5 for 'realistic' and 8.0 for 'heroic', and derive HEAD_UNIT from it. Keep 6.8 available as a 'tabletop mini' preset since an oversized head survives 28 mm printing far better.
- [medium/transformative] **Re-anchor the nine torso ring z-fractions to the measured landmark ladder, then add four new rings.** — Retarget existing rings (as fraction of figure height, sole=0): crotch 0.500 (from 0.515), hip/trochanter 0.520 (from 0.563), waist 0.625 (from 0.660), ribs/10th-rib 0.672 (from 0.702), nipple line 0.728 (from 0.767), armpit 0.775, shoulder/acromion 0.815 (from 0.842), trap 0.855, neck-top 0.885. Then insert R_navel at 0.605, R_iliac at 0.575, R_clavicle at 0.795, and R_inframammary at 0.700. Torso goes from 9×16=144 to 13×16=208 cage verts; total cage 403 → ~467, still trivial for numpy.
- [medium/high] **Split the pelvis into a trochanter ring and an iliac-crest ring so `hips` widens at the correct height.** — New trochanter ring at z-frac 0.520 (level with the pubic symphysis) carries the widest half-width and is driven by hips_eff and glute_drive; the iliac-crest ring at 0.575 gets 0.85 × the trochanter half-width. Target full widths at the trochanter: male 0.190 H, female 0.220 H (current female is 0.247 H at the wrong ring, which is too wide AND too high). Push glute mass to columns 2–5 on the trochanter ring rather than on the current R1.
- [small/transformative] **Rebalance the gender composite so shoulders stay skeletally similar and the dimorphism comes from waist, trochanter and deltoid mass.** — Change `sh_eff` coupling from ×(1 − 0.14·gm) to ×(1 − 0.03·gm) (skeletal biacromial/stature is 23.01 vs 22.57 — ~2%). Change `hips_eff` from ×(1 + 0.13·gm) to ×(1 + 0.08·gm) applied at the trochanter ring only. Change `waist_eff` from ×(1 − 0.10·gm) to ×(1 − 0.16·gm). Add a new deltoid/trap volume term driven by gender at ×(1 − 0.22·gm) so male width becomes muscle. Verification targets: deltoid-width/height 0.270 male and 0.258 female (currently 0.346 / 0.273); shoulder-ring/hip-ring 1.40 male and 1.20 female (currently 1.49 / 0.86); waist/hip width 0.86 male and 0.74 female (currently 0.94 / 0.60).
- [small/high] **Fix hand and foot scale, and constrain foot length to forearm length.** — Hand length target 0.108 H (currently 0.077 H — increase by ~40%, from 0.52 to 0.82 head units). Foot length target 0.152 H (currently 0.175 H — reduce by ~13%). Then assert |foot_len − forearm_len| < 0.01 H at every slider setting (currently forearm 0.120 vs foot 0.164). Note for 28–32 mm printing: at 30 mm total height a correctly-scaled hand is 3.2 mm long, so fingers are not printable at FDM resolution but are fine at 0.03 mm-layer resin — keep mitten hands as a print preset and add fingers as a display-quality option.
- [medium/high] **Add per-column z-offset tables to the limb rings so medial/lateral muscle bellies stagger vertically instead of sharing a ring plane.** — The leg tube already has 8 columns at ±22.5°/±67.5°. On the calf ring (L3, currently z 0.205, which is correctly at ~25% down the shank): raise the two lateral columns by +0.010 H and lower the two medial columns by −0.012 H. On the ankle ring (L5): raise the medial column by +0.006 H, lower the lateral by −0.006 H (medial malleolus is higher). On the thigh (L1): raise lateral (vastus lateralis) by +0.015 H relative to medial (vastus medialis). On the arm, terminate the deltoid V at the deltoid tuberosity just above the humeral midpoint — the existing S2 ring at z 0.700 already sits at that midpoint, so give S2 a per-column profile that is a deltoid-insertion notch laterally and a biceps belly anteriorly, and add one more ring at 40% down the humerus for the biceps peak.
- [small/high] **Introduce joint-angle dimorphism: elbow carrying angle and knee valgus.** — In joint_layout(), currently elbow_x = sh_x + 0.006 and wrist_x = sh_x + 0.012 — the arm is nearly a straight vertical column. Set the forearm axis to deviate laterally by the carrying angle: 8° at gender=male, 15° at gender=female (measured means 10.97° ± 4.27 vs 15.07° ± 4.95). For the leg, currently hip_x = 0.045·(...) and knee_x = 0.043, ankle_x = 0.040 — near-parallel. Set the femoral shaft to converge so the knee sits medially by tan(Q)·thigh_len with Q = 13° male, 16° female (published Q-angle ~12–13.5° vs ~15.9–17°). Add ~4° extra lumbar lordosis at the female end (27.6° vs 23.7° measured) by moving the waist and navel rings ~0.008 H forward and the sacrum ~0.006 H back.
- [medium/high] **Replace the scalar `weight` inflation with a gender-blended fat-deposition mask, and relabel it as body-fat percentage.** — Define two per-ring, per-column deposition weight vectors. ANDROID (male, weight applies at gender=0): neck/submental 0.4, chest 0.5, waist 1.0, navel 1.2, iliac 0.8, upper arm 0.4, thigh 0.3, glute 0.3. GYNOID (female, gender=1): neck 0.15, chest 0.3, waist 0.4, navel 0.5, iliac 0.7, trochanter 1.0, glute 1.1, thigh 1.0, upper arm 0.6. Blend by gender, then scale by the fat slider. Remap the slider to body-fat % using the ACE bands so the extremes mean something: male 5%→25%+ and female 13%→32%+, with the neutral default at 18% male / 25% female. Replaces the current uniform ws = 1 + 0.30·w plus front/back split.
- [medium/high] **Rebuild the bust as a three-ring anatomical structure anchored to rib levels.** — Upper pole at the 2nd-rib level (z-frac ~0.775, the existing armpit ring), apex at the 4th-rib level (z-frac ~0.728, the retargeted nipple ring), inframammary crease at the 6th-rib level (new ring, z-frac ~0.700). As bust_drive goes 0→1.25: move the apex laterally from column 11.5/12.5 toward 11/13 (nipple migrates lateral to the midclavicular line), drop the apex z by up to 0.020 H, and deepen the crease by up to 0.010 H in −y. Currently bust is one ring, fixed columns 10–13, fixed height.
- [large/transformative] **Formalize the gender composite into a named macro-vector basis, in the SMPL/MakeHuman spirit.** — Today `resolve()` hand-codes ~16 couplings inside one function. Restructure so each macro (gender, body-fat, muscle, proportion/head-count, age) is a dense vector of per-ring/per-column deltas of the same shape as the 403-vert cage, applied additively and clamped, with the 22 face sliders and the body detail sliders layered on top. That is the MakeHuman piecewise-multilinear architecture and the SMPL 10-beta architecture expressed in the code you already have; it makes a 'randomize plausible human' button and future age/ethnicity macros nearly free, and it keeps the coupling logic linear in slider count instead of quadratic.
- [small/medium] **Add a proportion regression test that pins the anatomical indices across the full slider space.** — Assert over a grid of slider extremes: total height / head = target ± 0.15; crotch at 0.50 ± 0.02 of height; knee joint at 0.28 ± 0.015; nipple line at 0.73 ± 0.02; acromion at 0.815 ± 0.015; brachial index (forearm/upper arm) in 0.74–0.80; crural index (tibia/femur) in 0.80–0.86; arm span within 3% of height; shoulder-ring/hip-ring in 1.15–1.45; hand length 0.108 ± 0.01 H; foot length 0.152 ± 0.012 H; |foot − forearm| < 0.01 H. /home/user/MiniMaster/tools/anatomy_eval.py already exists and is the natural home for this.

### Uncertainties

- WebFetch returned HTTP 403 on EVERY URL I tried (proko.com, makingcomics.com, humankinetics.com, wikipedia.org, fiveable.me, thedrawingsource.com, lovelifedrawing.com, anatomy4sculptors.com, skyryedesign.com, dokumen.pub). Every external number below therefore comes from search-result summaries, not from reading the primary page. The numbers I am most confident in are the ones I MEASURED myself by importing /home/user/MiniMaster/minimaster/character/generator.py and computing — those are exact.
- I could not obtain the ANSUR II summary-statistics table. Three CSV mirror URLs I probed returned 404 and the DTIC PDF (ADA611869) was not fetchable. So I have no per-percentile biacromial/bideltoid/bitrochanteric distributions — only the point estimates quoted in secondary sources.
- The biacromial:biiliac ratio '≈1.4 male, ≈1.2 female (US adults)' came from an encyclopedia-style search summary without a traceable primary citation. Hughes & Gallup's 1.18/1.03 uses a different protocol. Treat 1.4/1.2 as directionally right but verify before hard-coding.
- Bideltoid shoulder breadth: one search summary gave 46.7 cm for US men, and a second summary confusingly gave 46.7 cm as the FEMALE 50th percentile too. The male figure is consistent with other sources; the female figure is almost certainly a transcription error in that page and I did not verify a female bideltoid mean. My female shoulder-width target of 0.258 H is derived from the Korean biacromial/stature ratio plus an assumed deltoid allowance, not measured directly.
- Standing pelvic-tilt sex difference: the study I found reported 7.8° female vs 14.3° male, but its title says females showed GREATER anterior pelvic tilt. The sign convention in that paper is ambiguous from the summary alone. The lumbar-lordosis direction (greater in females) is corroborated by multiple sources, so I recommend driving the female curve from lordosis, not from the pelvic-tilt number.
- I found no primary source stating an explicit numeric rule for where the deltoid tuberosity sits along the humerus beyond 'a bit above the middle point'. My '50% of humerus length' target is that qualitative statement converted to a number.
- No source gave a hard number for how much of the anterior pelvic tilt / lordosis difference translates into surface silhouette displacement. My 0.008 H / 0.006 H offsets are engineering estimates derived from a ~4° angle over a ~0.15 H lumbar span, not measured values.
- Print-size implications for 28-32 mm miniatures (fingers, inframammary crease depth, per-column staggers of 0.006-0.015 H = 0.18-0.45 mm at 30 mm scale) are my own arithmetic against typical resin layer heights, not sourced. Several of the fine anatomical staggers recommended here will be below FDM resolution and near the limit of resin resolution — they matter for the realistic viewing target the user asked for, less so for the printed mini.

## Artistic anatomy of the head and face for 3D modeling — skull-driven structure, planes, canonical proportions with hard numbers, per-feature construction, sex dimorphism, and the specific geometric cues that separate a real-reading CG face from a mannequin. Findings are cross-checked against MiniMaster's actual `_HEAD_RINGS` table (minimaster/character/generator.py:345-357) and `subdivide_quads` (minimaster/core/subdiv.py:83).

### Findings

- **MiniMaster's eye line sits ~4.3% of head height too low, and the mouth ~4.6% too low — two of the most recognizable 'child/alien/mannequin' proportion errors.**
  - Canon: the eye line is at the vertical midpoint of the head (chin to vertex), and the lower third splits 30:70 at the stomion (subnasale->stomion : stomion->menton = 1:2). Normalizing the repo's `_HEAD_RINGS` z-values with chin=r1(0.11)=0.0 and crown pole=1.03=1.0 (span 0.92): eye ring r7 = (0.53-0.11)/0.92 = 0.457 (canon 0.500); mouth ring r3 = 0.152 (canon 0.198 = 0.283*0.70 where subnasale r5 = 0.283); lower-lip ring r2 = 0.098 (canon ~0.155). Brow r8 = 0.565 (canon ~0.55, fine) and nose base r5 = 0.283 (canon ~0.30, fine). So the brow-to-eye gap is 0.108 of head height where reality is ~0.05 — more than double.
  - *Implication:* These are pure table edits in `_HEAD_RINGS`, zero topology change, and they fix the single loudest non-realism signal. Corrected z-values: r2 0.20->0.252, r3 0.25->0.292, r4 0.30->0.318, r5 0.37->0.385, r6 0.46->0.515, r7 0.53->0.570, r8 0.63->0.617.
  - Source: https://www.proko.com/course-lesson/how-to-draw-the-head-from-any-angle
- **A pure latitude/longitude ring-and-column loft structurally cannot produce a realistic eye, nose or mouth — the face requires CONCENTRIC loops around orbicularis oculi and orbicularis oris.**
  - Production guidance is unanimous: 'primary loops around the eyes and mouth are your anchor; all other topology connects to them.' For animation, 3-4 circular loops around the eye orbit (2 minimum for low-poly mobile), and at least 2 loops around the eye opening — one along the lid margin, one set back for volume — plus a separate loop for the upper-lid crease, which is its own deformation zone. Loops run eye socket -> over brow -> down across cheek -> mouth corner -> back along jawline to ear. Edge flow must follow musculature, not surface contour.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's 11x16 head is 176 quads of horizontal rings. No amount of slider tuning gives it an eyelid margin, a lid crease, an alar crease, a vermilion border, or a nasolabial fold, because there are no edges running along those curves. This is the hard ceiling on the current head.
  - Source: https://vsquad.art/blog/modeling-guide-to-achieving-good-face-topology
- **Realistic head cages in production run roughly 5-40x MiniMaster's 176 quads; ~900-1,400 cage quads is the realistic minimum for feature loops.**
  - Surveyed head base meshes: one common head model is 7,392 quads at its lowest subdiv level; stylized/cartoon base meshes cluster at 1,684-2,476 quads. Guidance: keep quad density fairly uniform but add density where features are (eyes, nose, ears), and avoid 5+ poles running into each other because triangles and poles cause pinching under subdivision.
  - *Implication:* Sets a concrete rebuild budget. 176 -> ~1,000 quads is a ~6x cage, still trivial for numpy, and one subdivision level cheaper at render time (a level-2 subdiv of 1,000 quads ≈ 16k quads, comparable to level-3 of 176 = 11k).
  - Source: https://www.cgtrader.com/3d-models/character/human-anatomy/default-human-head-basemesh-lowpoly
- **Catmull-Clark semi-sharp creases (DeRose et al. 1998) are the cheapest possible way to make plane breaks read — and MiniMaster's subdivider has no crease support at all.**
  - Each control edge gets a sharpness sigma in R>=0: sigma=0 uses the smooth rules, sigma>=1 applies sharp/crease/corner rules for that iteration (decrementing sigma by 1 each level), 0<sigma<1 blends the two rule sets. Implementation: sharp edge -> edge point is the plain midpoint (no face-point averaging); a vertex on exactly 2 creased edges uses the crease rule (1/8, 6/8, 1/8 along the crease); a vertex on 3+ creased edges is a corner (stays put). Verified absent in `_cc_once` (minimaster/core/subdiv.py:83-89), which is uniform smooth Catmull-Clark.
  - *Implication:* Sharp, light-catching plane breaks are exactly what a smoothed loft destroys. Adding a per-edge sigma array is ~40 lines in subdiv.py and immediately buys crisp eyelid margins, nostril rims, vermilion borders, alar creases and ear helices without more cage verts.
  - Source: https://team.inria.fr/imagine/files/2014/10/subdivision_animation.pdf
- **The Asaro planar head reduces the face to ~41 flat planes and names exactly where realism lives; the front-plane/side-plane transition is the single most load-bearing line.**
  - John Asaro (1976) plane groups: cranium 8 planes, forehead-and-brow band 6, cheek-and-jaw mass 12, central feature mass (nose/mouth/chin) 15. The critical rhythm line runs from the corner of the mouth, past the corner of the nose, up to the side plane of the head at the brow ridge. Side planes wrap temple->cheek; top plane is forehead/upper skull; bottom plane is under the chin and jaw. Sculpting guidance: 'making planes too soft and rounded loses the structural clarity that catches light' — check plane transitions with a flat material shader and one sharp directional light.
  - *Implication:* Gives a concrete edge-placement checklist for the rebuilt cage: put a cage edge loop along (a) temple->zygomatic->mouth-corner->chin, (b) the brow shelf where top plane meets front plane, (c) the jaw/submental bottom plane. And MiniMaster already has a flat-shading rasterizer — that IS the Asaro check rig.
  - Source: https://gridmakerpro.com/learn/asaro-head/
- **Bizygomatic (cheekbone) WIDTH is the strongest facial sex dimorphism known (d = 1.39); facial width-to-height RATIO is a statistically spurious measure.**
  - In n=6068 men and women, adjusting for 92 allometric measurements, bizygomatic width was highly sexually dimorphic at equivalent d = 1.39. By contrast fWHR (bizygomatic width / upper facial height) 'demonstrated a statistical pattern consistent with the argument that morphological ratio measurements may engender spurious correlations and biased effect-size estimates.' Reported fWHR ranges: 1.589-2.15 (US sample), 1.806-2.265 (Turkish sample).
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's gender slider should drive absolute mid-face width at ring r6 (and its allometric coupling to overall head size), not a width/height ratio. This is the highest-signal single axis in the whole gender composite.
  - Source: https://royalsocietypublishing.org/rsbl/article/18/10/20220211/63021/Beyond-facial-width-to-height-ratios-bizygomatic
- **The gonial angle is only weakly (and inconsistently) sexually dimorphic — the male 'square jaw' comes from bigonial WIDTH, ramus mass and chin form, not the angle.**
  - Gonial angle in humans ranges 100-148 deg. Meta-level findings conflict: 'in all racial groups the mean angle in females is 3 to 5 degrees higher than in males' (i.e. the OPPOSITE of the intuition that males have sharper angles is partly true — females are slightly more obtuse); another study reports males 127.27 +/- 5.29 vs females 127.08 +/- 5.18 with p non-significant. Chin, by contrast, is clearly dimorphic: males have broader, taller, more protrusive chins with mental tubercles set further apart (square); females narrower, rounder, less protrusive.
  - *Implication:* If MiniMaster's gender coupling sharpens the gonial angle for males, it is modelling a myth. Reallocate that budget to bigonial width, masseter/ramus fullness, and a `chin_square` axis (tubercle spacing) separate from `chin_width`.
  - Source: https://healthcare-bulletin.co.uk/article/correlation-of-gonial-angle-with-age-sex-and-dental-status-of-an-individual-2613/
- **Male/female upper-face difference is a discrete brow-ridge SHAPE change, not a smooth scalar — Ousterhout's forehead types give the geometry.**
  - Ousterhout: the male forehead has forward projection, supraorbital bossing, and a small flat recess ~2 cm above the nasion; the female forehead is convex, smooth, and LACKS that recess. FFS types: Type I = mild bossing, thick anterior sinus wall, treat by burring only; Type II = moderate bossing with the concave supranasal recess, burr low + augment upper forehead; Type III = bossing from the curvature of the anterior table of the frontal sinus, requires cutting and repositioning bone. Mean planned anterior-table setback: 4.12 mm. Separately: the eyebrow overlies the orbital rim in males but sits several mm above it in females, and is straighter/more horizontal in males vs arched in females.
  - *Implication:* Gives a precise recipe for a `brow_ridge` slider distinct from `brow_height`: at male=1 add a forward glabellar shelf plus a concave notch ~2cm-equivalent (0.087 local units) above nasion; at female=1 flatten to a single convex curve AND raise the eyebrow soft-tissue band a few mm above the rim.
  - Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11455672/
- **Hard nose numbers: nasofrontal angle 120-130 deg male / 115-125 deg female; nasolabial angle ~95 deg male / ~105 deg female; Goode tip projection 0.55-0.60.**
  - Nasofrontal angle ideal range 115-130 deg overall, with population variation (Iranian 3D study preferred 137.64 +/- 4.20 male, 133.55 +/- 4.53 female). Nasolabial: ideal male 90.7-103.3 deg, female 100.9-108.9 deg; most-aesthetic values female 104.9 +/- 4.0, male 97.0 +/- 6.3; a Japanese cohort rated 95 deg (male) and 105 deg (female) best. Goode ratio: tip projection (alar crease to tip-defining point) should be 0.55-0.60 of nasal length (nasion to tip-defining point). Crumley-Lanser alternative: projection:height:length = 3:4:5. Byrd-Hobar: projection = 2/3 of nasal length, which is itself 2/3 of midfacial height.
  - *Implication:* Replaces MiniMaster's five ad-hoc nose sliders (bridge_height, bridge_depth, length, width, tip) with parameters that are directly measurable and gender-coupled. `nose_tip` should be reparameterized as nasolabial angle in degrees; `nose_bridge_depth` as nasofrontal angle; tip projection should auto-derive from Goode 0.55-0.60 * nasal length rather than being an independent unbounded offset.
  - Source: https://www.rhinologyonline.org/Rhinology_online_issues/manuscript_65.pdf
- **MiniMaster's eyeballs are ~17% oversized and its eye construction is missing globe protrusion, canthal tilt, and lid thickness.**
  - Real: eyeball 24.0 mm long x 23.7 high x 24.2 wide; iris 12 mm; cornea 11.5 mm wide, 0.5 mm thick. Globe protrusion from the lateral orbital rim (exophthalmometry): mean 16.1 +/- 2.6 mm male, 15.5 +/- 2.6 mm female (not significantly different); White men upper limit 21.7 mm, White women 20.1 mm, Black men 24.7 mm, Black women 23.0 mm. Canthal tilt: population mean ~4.12 deg (Caucasian); ideal ~+3 deg (2.1 mm) male, ~+4 deg (4.1 mm) female; surgeons propose 1-2 deg male, 3-4 deg female; celebrity-rated faces averaged 8.5 deg (women), 6.5 deg (men). Negative tilt is -5 to -8 deg. Rendering guidance: the missing CATCHLIGHT (specular highlight) is a named, top-tier cue that eyes read dead.
  - *Implication:* generator.py:449 uses eyeball radius 0.056 * Hh. With the head spanning 0.92 local units for a real ~230 mm chin-to-vertex, 1.0 local ~= 250 mm, so 0.056 = 14 mm radius = 28 mm diameter. Correct value is 0.048 (12 mm radius). Iris diameter should equal the eyeball RADIUS (12 mm = 0.048 local), palpebral fissure width ~30 mm = 0.120 local, and the cornea apex should sit 0.064 local in front of the lateral orbital rim plane. MiniMaster's renderer has no specular term at all — adding one just for the cornea is a few lines and is disproportionately high-impact.
  - Source: https://eyewiki.org/Exophthalmometry
- **Hard mouth numbers: vermilion lower:upper = 1:1.6 (phi), with measured heights ~6.5 mm upper / ~10.5 mm lower — and lip fullness is much LESS sex-dimorphic than assumed.**
  - Phi ideal: the lower lip should be 1.6x thicker than the upper, and longer. Measured (Indonesian cohort): upper vermilion height (ls-st) 5.60 +/- 1.60 mm male vs 6.59 +/- 1.39 mm female, NO significant gender difference; lower vermilion 10.51 +/- 2.09 mm male vs 10.02 +/- 1.38 mm female, NO significant difference. Philtrum height (sn-ls) 15.85 +/- 2.67 mm male vs 13.86 +/- 2.32 mm female (another study: 15.7 +/- 1.7 vs 14.3 +/- 1.9) — philtrum IS dimorphic. Structure: the cupid's bow peaks (Glogau-Klein points) coincide with the philtral columns; the philtrum runs from the bow to the nasal septum.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's single `lip_full` slider moves upper and lower by nearly the same amount (0.022 vs 0.026) — close to 1:1.2, not 1:1.6. Fix the ratio, and move the gender coupling OFF lip thickness and ONTO philtrum length (which is the actually-dimorphic quantity). Total lip height ~16.5 mm = 0.066 local, split 0.026 above stomion / 0.040 below.
  - Source: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10825920/
- **Ear placement rule is exact and MiniMaster's 2-row ear bump cannot satisfy it.**
  - The top of the ear aligns with the brow ridge, the bottom with the base of the nose — the ear lives entirely in the middle facial third. It tilts 15-20 deg from vertical with the top leaning toward the back of the head. Horizontally it sits directly behind the jaw; the jawline terminates at the ear base. Six structures an artist must read: helix (outer posterosuperior rim), antihelix (Y-shaped ridge with two crura bounding the conchal bowl), tragus, antitragus, concha, lobe.
  - *Implication:* With the corrected ring table (brow z=0.617, nose base z=0.385) the ear must span 0.231 local = ~58 mm, which matches real adult ear length (60-65 mm). Currently the ear is a vertex nudge on rows 6 and 7 only (z 0.46-0.53 = 0.07 local = 18 mm) — roughly a third of the correct height and in the wrong vertical band.
  - Source: https://www.leondavey.co.uk/blog/eyes-ears-nose-placement/
- **The uncanny-valley literature says photorealism RAISES the precision requirement, and that inconsistent realism across parts is itself a cause of uncanniness.**
  - 'Atypical facial proportions are more disturbing on photorealistic faces than on other faces'; less realistic, lower-poly faces tolerate a wider range of acceptable variation. A Cognition study found that REDUCING consistency in human realism increases the uncanny valley effect (while increasing category uncertainty does not). Deliberate subtle asymmetry is used as an active mitigation — 'automatic asymmetry helps to overcome the uncanny valley,' and slight imperfections are more appealing than perfectly smooth skin or perfect symmetry. Mannequins escape the valley precisely because they are abstract.
  - *Implication:* Two direct consequences for MiniMaster: (1) a photoreal head bolted to a mitten-handed, fingerless body is the exact 'inconsistent realism' failure mode — the head upgrade must be accompanied by hands or the whole figure gets WORSE; (2) add a per-character asymmetry seed (mirrored-cage + small deterministic L/R deltas) rather than exact mirror symmetry.
  - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010027715300755
- **Form hierarchy: primary and secondary forms carry realism; tertiary detail cannot rescue bad structure — which is good news for a numpy-only, textureless pipeline.**
  - Primary = largest basic shapes; secondary = muscle forms and flesh folds; tertiary = pores, fine wrinkles. 'For a sculpture to work, the primary and secondary forms are the most important; the tertiary forms are just icing on the cake and cannot make a bad sculpture good.' The classic failure is jumping to eyes/lips before the skull structure is right, producing a 'mask-like' face lacking volume. Conversely, the named reasons CG SKIN looks fake are all tertiary/shading: absent pores, absent subsurface scattering, absent catchlight — 'four factors separate realistic-looking skin from fake: albedo, displacements, subsurface scattering, and dynamic changes.'
  - *Implication:* Confirms the correct build order for MiniMaster: skull-driven primary forms + Asaro plane breaks + secondary forms (malar fat pad, buccal hollow, nasolabial fold, mentolabial sulcus) FIRST. Textureless flat/gouraud shading is not the blocker for geometric realism — but it IS the ceiling on final believability, so a cheap wrap-diffuse + rim + corneal specular is the highest-value render upgrade.
  - Source: https://what-when-how.com/zbrush-character-creation-advanced-digital-sculpting/form-and-details-zbrush-for-detailing-zbrush-character-creation/
- **Secondary facial forms have named anatomy that maps directly to cage loops: malar fat pad (superficial), buccal fat pad (deep), nasolabial fold, mentolabial sulcus.**
  - The malar fat pad lies directly beneath cheek skin and IS the surface contour; the buccal fat pad lies outside buccinator and deep to masseter/zygomaticus major and minor, and its atrophy produces hollow cheeks and deepens the nasolabial fold. The nasolabial (melolabial) fold runs from the lateral nasal ala to a point lateral to the mouth corner, and exists because levator labii, the zygomatic head, and orbicularis all insert into the skin along that line. The mentolabial sulcus contains no fat — it is fibres of mentalis, depressor labii inferioris, and orbicularis oris. The zygomatic bone is diamond-shaped; its convex anterolateral surface is the malar eminence; it joins the temporal bone to form the zygomatic arch. 'High-set cheekbones with a visible submalar hollow' are the strong aesthetic read.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster already has cheek_height/cheek_depth/cheek_full, which is the right decomposition (bone height, bone projection, soft-tissue volume) — the user's request 'depth of cheekbones and where they are, affecting facial muscles' is literally malar projection + malar height + malar fat pad volume. What's missing is a SUBMALAR HOLLOW axis (buccal fat) as the signed opposite, and a nasolabial fold edge loop to receive it.
  - Source: https://plasticsurgerykey.com/the-malar-region/
- **At 28-32 mm print scale, most real facial detail is physically unprintable — realism and printability need separate geometry passes.**
  - Resin printer XY resolution for miniatures: usable range ~20-35 microns, sweet spot 17-24 microns; layer height 0.05 mm for tabletop, 0.03 mm display, 0.02 mm competition. 'The difference between a face that reads as human and a featureless blob is about 20 microns of XY detail.' Scale math: a 32 mm figure at 7.5 heads gives a 4.27 mm head; a real 233 mm head means a scale of ~1:55. A 0.5 mm real alar crease becomes 9 microns — below resolution. A 2 mm nasolabial fold becomes 36 microns — marginal. To land at 20-35 microns a feature must be ~1.1-1.9 mm deep in real-world terms.
  - *Implication:* Justifies a `detail_exaggeration` scalar (recommend 2.5-3.5x on all plane-break depths and crease displacements) applied ONLY on the STL export path, with 1.0 on the render path. Without it a geometrically perfect realistic head prints as a smooth blob at 28 mm; with it the printed mini reads correctly while the render stays anatomically honest.
  - Source: https://store.heygears.com/blogs/blog/warhammer-miniatures-how-to-choose-the-best-resin-3d-printer-for-28mm-models
- **MetaHuman — the user's named benchmark — is a hybrid joint + FACS-blendshape rig with a data-driven identity space, not a slider-offset system.**
  - Reported: ~700-800 facial joints in the face skeletal mesh, plus 710 FACS-driven blendshapes, running on top of standard skinning rather than replacing it. A binary .dna file holds the rig logic that maps a few hundred semantic control inputs to joint transforms and blendshape weights; control curves follow a fixed naming scheme (CTRL_L_brow_raiseIn, CTRL_C_jaw_open). The identity-space analogue in open research is the 3D Morphable Model: the Basel Face Model is a PCA over 3D scans of 200 adult faces, with shape = mean mesh + sum(alpha_i * eigenvector_i); BFM-2017 adds a broader age distribution and a statistical expression subspace.
  - *Implication:* Two lessons. (1) MetaHuman-grade deformation quality comes from a per-vertex delta BASIS, not from hand-authored per-slider vertex nudges — MiniMaster's 22 facial sliders should be refactored into a shared 'add a named delta field' mechanism so new sliders are data, not code. (2) A PCA/morphable identity axis (even a hand-authored 8-12 archetype basis blended by weights) gives correlated, plausible faces where independent sliders give implausible ones — this is the difference between 'deep customization' and 'a pile of knobs'.
  - Source: https://medium.com/@Jamesroha/metahuman-5-6-5-7-pipeline-reference-170d302b078e

Add a corneal specular highlight to the rasterizer even if the rest stays diffuse — absent catchlight is a named, top-tier 'looks fake' cue.
- [medium/medium] **Give the ear real geometry spanning brow to nose base with correct tilt.** — Ear vertical span: top at the brow ring, bottom at the nose-base ring. With the corrected table that is z 0.385 to 0.617 = 0.231 local = ~58 mm, matching real adult ear length (60-65 mm). Current implementation only nudges rows 6-7 (z 0.46-0.53 = 0.07 local = ~18 mm), about a third of correct. Tilt the ear axis 15-20 deg from vertical, top toward the rear. - [medium/high] **Split cheek control into malar bone vs soft tissue with a signed submalar hollow — this is precisely what the user asked for.** — Keep the existing three-way decomposition (cheek_height = malar eminence vertical position, cheek_depth = malar anterolateral projection, cheek_full = malar fat pad volume) but add a fourth: `submalar_hollow`, signed, driving the buccal-fat region between the zygomatic arch and the mandible. Positive = hollow/gaunt (buccal fat atrophy), negative = full. Add a nasolabial fold edge loop from the lateral ala to a point lateral to the mouth corner so the hollow has an edge to terminate against, and a mentolabial sulcus loop below the lower lip. - [medium/high] **Refactor the 22 facial sliders from bespoke per-slider code into a data-driven named-delta-field system, and layer a small correlated identity basis on top.** — Today generator.py:389-436 is ~48 lines of hand-written per-slider vertex arithmetic; every new slider is new code. Replace with a table of (slider_name, vertex_group, delta_vector, scale) rows evaluated in a loop, so adding 'nose_bridge_width' or 'orbital_rim_depth' is one row. Then add an identity layer above it: 8-12 hand-authored archetype heads expressed as full-cage delta arrays, blended by weight — this is the poor-man's version of the Basel Face Model (mean mesh + sum of alpha_i * eigenvector_i over a PCA of 200 scans) and of MetaHuman's .dna control mapping (a few hundred semantic controls -> joint transforms + 710 FACS blendshape weights). Correlated archetypes produce plausible faces; 22 independent knobs produce implausible ones. - [small/medium] **Add a deterministic per-character asymmetry seed.** — Build the cage symmetric, then apply small seeded left/right deltas before subdivision: suggested magnitudes as fractions of head height — eye height +/-0.004 (1 mm), eye size +/-1.5%, mouth corner height +/-0.003, nose tip lateral +/-0.002, ear height +/-0.004, jaw width +/-1%. Justification: 'automatic asymmetry helps to overcome the uncanny valley' and slight imperfections read better than perfect symmetry, while perfectly symmetric faces read as mannequins.
- [small/medium] **Add a print-only detail exaggeration pass, separate from the render path.** — Scale factor math: a 32 mm figure at 7.5 heads gives a 4.27 mm head vs a real 233 mm head = ~1:55. Implement `detail_gain` defaulting to 1.0 on the render/preview path and 2.5-3.5 on the STL export path, multiplying all crease displacements and plane-break depths (not overall proportions). - [large/high] **Do not ship a realistic head onto the current mitten-handed body — consistency of realism is itself a measured uncanny-valley driver.** — Experimental finding: reducing consistency in human realism increases the uncanny valley effect (whereas increasing category uncertainty does not). Sequence the work so the head rebuild lands together with, at minimum, separated fingers on the hands and a foot with toe separation, or gate the new head behind a `realism_level` flag that also raises body detail. Concretely, pair the ~1,000-quad head with a hand upgrade from the current 8-vert palm/knuckle tube to five digit tubes (roughly 4 rings x 8 columns each, ~150 quads per hand).
- [small/high] **Add three cheap shading terms that disproportionately break the mannequin read.** — The named reasons CG skin reads as fake are albedo variation, displacement, subsurface scattering, and catchlight. Within a pure-numpy rasterizer with no textures, three approximations are affordable: (1) wrap/half-Lambert diffuse (N.L mapped through (N.L*0.5+0.5)^2) to fake SSS light bleed into terminators; (2) a warm-tinted rim/backscatter term at grazing angles, strongest on the ears and nose alae where real ears glow red in backlight; (3) a specular lobe applied ONLY to the cornea and lower lip. - I could NOT verify absolute Farkas millimetre norms for intercanthal distance (en-en) and alar width (al-al) split by sex for North American Caucasians. Treat the orbitonasal canon (alar width = intercanthal distance) as an artistic default, not a measured mean.
- Absolute bizygomatic width in mm by sex is NOT verified. The d=1.39 effect size is solid (n=6068, allometry-adjusted), but I did not obtain male/female means, so I cannot tell you how many mm the gender slider should move ring r6. Needs one more lookup before hard-coding.
- Gonial angle sexual dimorphism is genuinely contested in the literature I found: one source says female means run 3-5 deg HIGHER across racial groups, another reports 121.35 female vs 120.13 male, a third reports 127.27 male vs 127.08 female with p non-significant. My recommendation to de-emphasize the angle follows from that inconsistency, but it is an inference, not a settled result.
- Philtrum length numbers conflict badly across sources: ~20 mm women / 22 mm men attributed to Farkas, vs 11-15 mm in aesthetic guidance, vs measured 15.85 mm male / 13.86 mm female (Indonesian) and 15.7 / 14.3 (unspecified). The spread is a measurement-definition problem (to vermilion border vs mid-philtral height) plus population. Use the ~15.7/14.3 pair and treat the direction of the sex difference as the reliable part.
- The Asaro plane counts (41 total; cranium 8, forehead/brow 6, cheek/jaw 12, feature mass 15) come from a single tutorial site (gridmakerpro), not from Asaro or a primary publication. The regional breakdown should be treated as approximate.
- MetaHuman internals (~700-800 facial joints, 710 FACS blendshapes, .dna file structure) come from a Medium pipeline reference and Epic community forum posts, not from official Epic documentation, which WebFetch could not reach. Directionally right; exact counts unconfirmed.
- The claim that eyeballs at radius 0.056*Hh are ~17% oversized depends on my inference that 1.0 local unit ~= 250 mm (derived from the head spanning 0.92 local units and a real ~230 mm chin-to-vertex). If HEAD_UNIT is calibrated to a different real head height, rescale the 0.048 figure proportionally. I did not verify a canonical 230 mm chin-to-vertex measurement.
- The print-scale math (1:55, 9 microns for a 0.5 mm alar crease) is my arithmetic from a 32 mm / 7.5-head figure, not a cited result. The resin XY resolution band (20-35 microns usable, 17-24 sweet spot) IS from sources, but those are vendor and review blogs, not independent metrology.
- Vermilion height and lip-thickness sex non-difference comes from a single Indonesian cohort. The 'no significant gender difference' result may not generalize; the 1:1.6 lower:upper phi ratio is an aesthetic ideal rather than a population mean.
- Nasofrontal angle ideals vary widely by population (115-130 deg Western vs 133-138 deg in an Iranian 3D study). Any single default will be ethnically biased; consider exposing it and defaulting per an archetype rather than globally.
- I did not find a primary source for the exact vertical position of the malar eminence as a fraction of head height. My 0.44 normalized figure is interpolated from 'just below the lower orbital rim' plus the corrected eye line at 0.50, not measured.
- WebFetch was 403-blocked on every target I tried (pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov included), so all findings are extracted from search-result summaries rather than full-text reads. Numbers should be spot-checked against the linked sources before being hard-coded.

- **The .target format is trivially simple: ASCII, one line per affected vertex, `vertIndex dx dy dz`, sparse, 0-based, deltas in decimetres.**
  - Example from targets/nose/nose-hump-incr.target: `106 0 0 0` / `109 0 -.008 0` / `114 0 -.027 0`. Header is `#`-prefixed comments including a license block and `# basemesh hm08`. Parser is ~12 lines (core/algos3d.py, Target._load_text): skip `#`, split, require len==4, int index + 3 floats. The compiled binary form (_save_binary) is uint16 index + int16 vector of round(delta*1e3), i.e. deltas quantised to 0.001 dm = 0.1 mm, 8 bytes per affected vertex. Application is pure addition: `obj.coord[dstVerts] += self.data[srcVerts] * scale * morphFactor`.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's 38 sliders are hand-written vertex math. Switching to sparse deltas decouples slider authoring from code entirely: a slider becomes a data file. You can implement the whole loader in numpy in an afternoon, and then every new feature is a data change, not a code change. The int16*1e-3 quantisation is a free 4x compression you should copy verbatim.

- **The macro system is exact tensor-product multilinear interpolation over a categorical grid, with a piecewise-linear tent basis per axis. The arithmetic reconciles perfectly: 24+72+144+108 = 348 macro targets.**
  - MPFB2's targets/macrodetails/macro.json declares 8 macro axes and 5 combination groups. Axes and their breakpoints: gender {female,male} 1 segment; age {baby,child,young,old} with breakpoints at 0.1875 and 0.5 (3 segments); muscle {min,average,max} break at 0.5; weight {min,average,max} break at 0.5; height {min,-,max}; proportions {uncommon,-,ideal}; cupsize; firmness. _interpolate_macro_components() returns at most 2 (label, weight) pairs per axis via linear ramp. Combination groups multiply across axes: racegenderage = 3x2x4 = 24 files (african|asian|caucasian - male|female - baby|child|young|old); genderagemuscleweight (the `universal-*` files) = 2x4x3x3 = 72; genderagemuscleweightheight = 2x4x3x3x2 = 144; genderagemuscleweightproportions = 2x3x3x3x2 = 108 (no baby). 24+72+144+108 = 348, which is EXACTLY the file count in data/targets/macrodetails. Weight of each combination target = product of its per-axis weights, with a cutoff of 0.01. MakeHuman core does the same thing via humanmodifier.getTargetWeights(), which is `reduce(operator.mul, [factors[f] for f in tfactors])`. The tent functions are in human.py: babyVal = max(0, 1 - age*5.333); youngVal = max(0, (age-0.1875)*3.2); childVal = max(0, min(1, 5.333*age) - youngVal); maxweightVal = max(0, weight*2-1); minweightVal = max(0, 1-weight*2); averageweightVal = 1 - (max+min). Since at most 2 components are live per axis, at most 2^N targets are active — 32 max for the 5-axis height group, typically ~16.
  - *Implication:* This is the direct, principled replacement for MiniMaster's 'gender = a composite re-weighting of ~16 couplings'. Instead of hand-tuned couplings, you define N orthogonal axes, author corner shapes for the grid, and multiply. It is 20 lines of code, exactly reproducible, and adding a new axis is mechanical. Critically it explains WHY MakeHuman looks correct at extremes: a max-muscle max-weight old male is not a sum of independent offsets, it is a dedicated authored corner shape.

- **Proxy/alternative-topology fitting is barycentric-triangle + axis-scaled offset — 9 numbers per proxy vertex — and MakeHuman ships topologies from 741 to 13,868 verts using it.**
  - Format (.proxy / .mhclo, ASCII): a header with `x_scale v1 v2 den`, `y_scale`, `z_scale`, then one line per proxy vertex: `i0 i1 i2 w0 w1 w2 ox oy oz`. Example from proxy741: `6838 11483 11484 0.46185 0.23830 0.29985 -0.00007 -0.00043 0.00089`. Reconstruction (shared/proxy.py getCoords): coord = h[i0]*w0 + h[i1]*w1 + h[i2]*w2 + M @ offset, where M is diagonal with M[n][n] = |h[v1][n] - h[v2][n]| / den for the axis-n scale pair.   - Source: https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman-assets/tree/master/base/proxymeshes
- **MB-Lab uses a fundamentally different coupling algorithm: a 'fuzzy' additive/normalised corner blend, not a tensor product — and it stores the full corner grid explicitly.**
  - data/morphs/human_female_morphs.json holds 220 morphs, format {name: [[vertIdx, dx, dy, dz], ...]}; affected-vert counts range 13 to 18,210 (median 536). 68 of the 220 are COMBINED morphs across 14 families, named `Region_ParamA-ParamB_min-max`: Torso_Mass-Tone (4 corners), Neck_Mass-Tone (4), Shoulders_Mass-Tone (4), Abdomen_Mass-Tone (4), Hands_Mass-Tone (4), Arms_UpperarmMass-UpperarmTone (4), Arms_ForearmMass-ForearmTone (4), Legs_UpperlegsMass-UpperlegsTone (4), Legs_LowerlegsMass-LowerlegsTone (4), Torso_BreastMass-BreastPosX (4), Torso_BreastMass-BreastScaleY (4), and Torso_BreastMass-BreastTone-Mass-Tone which is a 4-parameter family with all 16 corners. The algorithm (algorithms.smart_combo + function_modifier_a/b in humanoid.combine_morphings): each param value x in [0,1] splits into a = max(0, 2x-1) ('max' branch) and b = max(0, 1-2x) ('min' branch); corner weight = SUM of branch values across params (not product); then w = max(0, w - best/1.5) to cull, then normalised so the set sums to max(branch maxima). MB-Lab base meshes are 18,210 verts for the realistic humans and 13,995 for anime. Joints are defined as vertex-index lists whose centroid gives the position (human_female_joints.json has 530 entries including IK controls; anime rigs 142).
  - *Implication:* MB-Lab's 'Mass/Tone' pair is exactly the coupling MiniMaster is trying to hand-tune with weight and muscle. The lesson is: author 4 corner shapes per coupled pair (min-min, min-max, max-min, max-max) and blend, rather than adding two independent deltas. Note MB-Lab's sum-then-normalise is lossier and weirder than MakeHuman's product — I'd copy MakeHuman's product form and MB-Lab's naming/authoring convention.
  - Source: https://github.com/animate1978/MB-Lab/blob/master/algorithms.py
- **Every asset — base mesh, all 1,280 targets, the rig, the weights, the proxies — is CC0 1.0 Universal as of September 2020. Only the source code is AGPL3 (GPL3 for MPFB2).**
  - LICENSE.md section C: 'The assets are defined as any data contributing to the graphical output... The base mesh and proxies / Targets and modifiers / Textures / Clothes (any MHCLO-based asset) / Poses and expressions. These assets have been released under CC0 1.0 Universal.' Every single .target file carries the inline header 'This asset was explicitly released as CC0 in september 2020.' default_weights.mhw has `"license": "CC0"`. proxy741.proxy carries the same CC0 header. Important caveat from the community docs: the CC0-for-EXPORTS exception applies only to models produced by an unmodified official MakeHuman GUI — that governs generated .mhm/.obj output, NOT the shipped assets themselves, which are unconditionally CC0.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster can ingest base.obj, the 243 facial targets, the 348 macro targets, the 163-bone rig and its 57,107 weight pairs directly into a closed-source, commercial, pure-numpy tool with zero attribution obligation and zero AGPL contamination — provided you write your own loader and do not copy MakeHuman's Python. This removes the single biggest objection to 'just use MakeHuman's data'.
  - Source: https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/blob/master/LICENSE.md
- **Two small support tables do a lot of work and are cheap to copy: a per-vertex symmetry map and vertex-path 'rulers' for real-world measurements.**
  - (1) MPFB2's mesh_metadata/hm08.mirror is 19,158 lines of `index mirrorIndex side` — 9,402 left, 9,402 right, 354 midline. That single file drives all L/R symmetry, mirroring, and 'apply to both sides' behaviour. (2) MakeHuman's measurement system (plugins/0_modeling_a_measurement.py, class Ruler) is just a dict of vertex-index polylines: neck-circ is a 42-index loop, bust-circ 44, waist-circ 46, hips-circ 34, thigh-circ 20, calf-circ 20, wrist-circ 20, ankle-circ 20, knee-circ 20; linear measures are 2-index pairs (upperarm-length [8274,10037], shoulder-dist [7478,8274], napetowaist [1491,4181], waisttohip [4121,4341]). Girth = summed segment lengths in decimetres * 10 -> cm. 20 measure modifiers pair each ruler with a decr/incr target.
  - *Implication:* Both are ~1 day of work each and both are things MiniMaster currently lacks. The ruler system in particular turns abstract sliders into real numbers a user can reason about ('waist 74 cm'), and for a 3D-print tool it also gives you a direct way to report and validate physical dimensions at print scale.
  - Source: https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/blob/master/makehuman/plugins/0_modeling_a_measurement.py
- **Character state serialisation is one line per slider, and target application is order-independent pure addition onto a reset mesh.**
  - The .mhm format (verified against mpfb2/test/testdata/testchar.mhm) is plain text: `modifier head/head-square 0.422000`, `modifier macrodetails/Gender 1.000000`, `modifier macrodetails-universal/Muscle 0.721000`, plus `skeleton default.mhskel`, `subdivide False`. human.applyAllTargets() does: algos3d.resetObj(mesh) to restore the pristine base, then loops the whole targetsDetailStack applying each with its factor (pure +=), THEN updates proxies, THEN updates skeleton joint positions from the morphed joint cubes, THEN subdivides. Subdivision is the last step and is optional/toggleable.
  - *Implication:* Confirms MiniMaster's 'deform cage then subdivide' ordering is correct and matches MakeHuman. It also gives you a free, human-readable save format and confirms that with additive sparse deltas you never need to worry about slider evaluation order — a real simplification over composite coupling code.
  - Source: https://github.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/blob/master/makehuman/apps/human.py
- **MPFB2 is the actively maintained successor and represents targets as native Blender shape keys; MakeHuman standalone is effectively in maintenance.**
  - MPFB 2.0.8 was the first stable MPFB2 release on the Blender extension platform, dated 2025-01-28 (per the MakeHuman community release page and CG Channel coverage). MPFB2 ships the byte-identical base.obj (1,749,303 bytes, same as MakeHuman's) and 1,258 gzipped .target.gz files plus 187 other target assets (1,445 files total). It adds mesh_metadata (face<->vertex tables, hm08.mirror, hm08_config.json, proxy_corrective.json), rigify + standard rig sets, and a custom-targets install path so user .target files appear as sliders. Targets are added as Blender shape keys rather than baked, so they stay live and animatable.
  - *Implication:* Two practical consequences. (1) MPFB2's data layout (macro.json, target.json, hm08_config.json) is a cleaner, more declarative spec than MakeHuman core's — read those three JSON files, not the AGPL Python, when designing MiniMaster's config schema. (2) 'targets as shape keys' is the same insight as 'sliders as sparse delta arrays kept live and summed at evaluation time' — which is what MiniMaster should adopt.
  - Source: https://static.makehumancommunity.org/mpfb/releases/release_208.html

### Recommendations

- [large/transformative] **Rebuild the head as a dedicated dense cage, not a 178-vertex shell. This is the prerequisite for everything the user asked for and nothing else matters until it's done.** — Target 1,200–2,000 control verts for the head (vs 178 today). Concretely: go from 11 rings x 16 columns to ~24 rings x 32 columns (768) for the cranium/jaw shell, PLUS dedicated feature loops that MakeHuman has and MiniMaster lacks: an eye loop (2 concentric rings x 16 = 32 verts per eye, giving upper/lower lid), a mouth loop (2 concentric rings x 20 = 40 verts giving separate upper/lower lip bands), a nasal loop (alar crease ring of 12 + dorsum column of 5), a nasolabial fold loop, and an ear as real geometry (~120 verts) instead of vertex bumps. MakeHuman's own budget for reference: nose region 741 verts, cheek 768, eyes 1,776, mouth 1,334. Aim for roughly 1/3 of MakeHuman's face budget as a first pass. Keep the true front midline column you already have — hm08 has 354 midline verts and it matters for symmetry.
- [medium/transformative] **Replace hand-written per-slider vertex math with a sparse morph-target system: `vertIndex dx dy dz` ASCII files, loaded into numpy, applied additively onto a reset cage.** — Loader is ~15 lines: skip lines starting with '#', split, require 4 fields, build `idx = np.array(int)` and `delta = np.array(float).reshape(-1,3)`. Apply: `coord[idx] += delta * weight`. Store compiled as uint16 index + int16 round(delta*1000) = 8 bytes per affected vertex, exactly MakeHuman's scheme. Evaluate as: `coord = base.copy(); for (idx, delta, w) in stack: if w: coord[idx] += delta * w`. Order-independent, so no dependency graph needed. Serialize state as one line per slider: `modifier nose/nose-width2 0.42`.
- [medium/transformative] **Adopt MakeHuman's slider taxonomy verbatim for the face — it is CC0, it is a solved problem, and it is precisely the vocabulary the user named.** — 21 nose sliders (trans down|up, trans backward|forward, trans in|out, scale-vert, scale-horiz, scale-depth, nostrils-width, point-width, base down|up, width1, width2, width3, compression, curve concave|convex, greek, hump, volume, nostrils-angle, point down|up, septumangle, flaring); 8 cheek (volume, bones, inner, trans down|up per side); 34 eye (bag, bag-height, eyefold-angle, epicanthus, eyefold concave|convex, eyefold down|up, height1/2/3, push1/2, trans, scale, corner1/2 per side); 22 mouth (scale h/v/d, trans x3, lowerlip-height/width/ext/middle/volume, upperlip-height/width/ext/middle/volume, cupidsbow, cupidsbow-width, philtrum-volume, dimples, laugh-lines, angles); 7 chin (jaw-drop, cleft, prominent, width, height, bones, prognathism); 4 forehead (trans backward|forward, scale-vert, nubian, temple); 22 ear. That is 118 facial sliders vs today's 22. The user's exact asks map to: nose-width1/2/3 + nose-greek + nose-hump + nose-curve (bridge height/width/profile), nose-scale-vert (length), cheek-bones + cheek-inner + cheek-volume + cheek-trans-up (cheekbone depth and vertical placement).
- [medium/high] **Replace the ad-hoc 'gender = ~16 couplings' composite with proper tensor-product multilinear macro blending over a categorical grid.** — Define axes with tent bases exactly as MakeHuman does: gender {female,male}; age {baby,child,young,old} with knots at 0.0/0.1875/0.5/1.0; muscle {min,average,max} knot at 0.5; weight {min,average,max} knot at 0.5; height {min,-,max}. Per axis emit at most 2 (label, weight) pairs by linear ramp. Then weight(corner) = product of per-axis weights, cull below 0.01, and sum the corresponding corner targets. Start small — a gender x weight x muscle grid is 2x3x3 = 18 corner shapes, versus MakeHuman's full 2x4x3x3 = 72 universal set. If you drop age entirely (miniatures are mostly adults), you need only 18 body corners, which is completely tractable to author.
- [small/high] **Add joint cubes to the cage and derive every bone head/tail as a cube centroid, so the 16-bone armature auto-tracks all proportion sliders.** — Add one 8-vertex cube (6 quads) per joint, embedded in the body but excluded from the render/STL face mask — MakeHuman uses 130 of them; you need ~18 for a 16-bone rig (pelvis, spine1-3, neck, head, l/r clavicle, shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle). Bone head = mean of its cube's 8 verts, computed AFTER morphs are applied and BEFORE skinning. Cost: ~144 extra cage verts and about 60 lines. This immediately fixes joint drift under height, leg_len, arm_len, torso_len, neck_len, and shoulders.
- [medium/high] **Introduce a barycentric proxy layer so the render/print mesh is decoupled from the morph mesh — this is how you get realistic faces AND a printable 28 mm STL.** — Store 9 numbers per output vertex: three source-mesh vertex indices, three barycentric weights summing to 1, and a 3-component offset. Reconstruct as `p = h[i0]*w0 + h[i1]*w1 + h[i2]*w2 + M @ offset`, where M is diagonal with M[n][n] = |h[a_n][n] - h[b_n][n]| / den_n for three chosen reference vertex pairs (MakeHuman uses e.g. `x_scale 5399 11998 1.3980`). Precedent: MakeHuman's proxy741 is 741 verts / 730 faces and tracks every slider off a 13,380-vert base. Fit the map once offline with a nearest-triangle search; runtime is three gathers and an add.
- [medium/transformative] **Ingest MakeHuman's CC0 assets directly instead of authoring targets from scratch — legally clean, and it is by far the fastest path to 'actual realistic'.** — Pull makehuman/data/3dobjs/base.obj (19,158 v / 18,486 quads) and the 243 facial .target files (~0.56 MB compiled) plus the 20 measure targets and the 163-bone default.mhskel with default_weights.mhw (57,107 weight pairs, explicitly `"license": "CC0"`). All carry the inline header 'explicitly released as CC0 in september 2020'. Write your own loader in numpy — do NOT copy any MakeHuman Python, which is AGPL3. Optionally take the 41.5 MB macrodetails set too, or regenerate a smaller grid yourself. If you'd rather keep your own topology, use the barycentric proxy layer above to retarget MakeHuman's deltas onto the MiniMaster cage.
- [medium/medium] **Copy MB-Lab's coupled-corner convention (not its blend math) for the parameter pairs that genuinely interact.** — For each coupled pair author 4 corner shapes named `Region_A-B_min-min`, `min-max`, `max-min`, `max-max` — MB-Lab does exactly this for Mass-Tone on Torso, Neck, Shoulders, Abdomen, Hands, Upperarm, Forearm, Upperlegs, Lowerlegs, and a 16-corner set for BreastMass-BreastTone-Mass-Tone. Map your weight/muscle pair onto it. But use MakeHuman's PRODUCT weighting (w = wA * wB) rather than MB-Lab's sum-then-cull-then-normalise (`smart_combo`: w = sum of branches, then max(0, w - best/1.5), then normalise) — the product form is partition-of-unity and better behaved.
- [small/medium] **Add a per-vertex symmetry table and a measurement ruler table — two small data files that unlock disproportionate functionality.** — Symmetry: one line per cage vertex, `index mirrorIndex side` with side in {l, m, r}. hm08's is 9,402/354/9,402 across 19,158. Generate yours automatically by matching (-x, y, z) within epsilon. Rulers: a dict of vertex-index polylines — girth loops of 20–46 indices (neck 42, bust 44, waist 46, hips 34, thigh 20, calf 20, knee 20, ankle 20, wrist 20, upperarm 20) and 2-index linear spans (shoulder-dist, upperarm-length, lowerarm-length, napetowaist, waisttohip, upperleg-height, lowerleg-height). Sum segment lengths and report in cm at 1:1 and in mm at print scale.
- [small/high] **Add a print-scale compensation policy for fine facial sliders, and be explicit with the user about what survives at 28–32 mm.** — Measured full-throw displacements scaled to a 32 mm figure: chin-bones 0.269 mm, cheek-volume 0.182 mm, nose-scale-depth 0.165 mm, nose-hump 0.086 mm, nose-width2 0.056 mm, nose-width1 0.044 mm. Bucket sliders by scaled throw: >0.15 mm = prints fine; 0.05–0.15 mm = marginal, apply a user-controllable exaggeration multiplier (1.5x–3x) on STL export; <0.05 mm = render-only, grey out or fold into a coarser parent slider when export scale is under ~40 mm. Also surface the scaled throw in the UI next to each slider so the user can see what they're buying.
- [small/medium] **Model MiniMaster's config schema on MPFB2's three declarative JSON files rather than inventing one.** — macro.json (axes with `parts` = list of {lowest, highest, low, high} tent segments, plus `combinations` naming which axes form each target grid); target.json (per-region `categories` with `label`, `has_left_and_right`, and an `opposites` map of positive/negative x left/right/unsided target names); hm08_config.json (`dimensions` for measurement vertex pairs, `select_groups` mapping logical parts to face groups, `groups_by_range` mapping names to vertex index ranges e.g. `body: [0, 13379]`). These three files are the whole parametric spec in ~700 lines of JSON, are GPL3-code-adjacent but describe CC0 data, and are a far better starting schema than growing MiniMaster's 38 hardcoded sliders.

## Commercial character creator architecture (Bethesda/FaceGen, Daz Genesis, Epic MetaHuman + Rig Logic, Reallusion CC4, MakeHuman, Hero Forge / Eldritch Foundry) — how they actually represent shape, how one slider drives many regions, how correctives work, and what that means for MiniMaster's cage+slider+Catmull-Clark generator.

### Findings

- **Every commercial creator I could verify represents shape as an explicit per-vertex DELTA BANK (morph targets) added to a neutral mesh — not as procedural arithmetic on ring parameters.**
  - Bethesda (FO4/Skyrim): .tri files are a FaceGen format holding the base triangle mesh plus named morphs that move each vertex; HeadChargen.tri specifically drives the chargen slider menu, and Fallout 4 ships 79 vanilla .tri files. 3ds Max import maps each .tri morph to one Morpher channel (100 channels/modifier). The format distinguishes 'regular morphs' (named 'MorphKey A [1]') from 'modifier morphs' ('ModifierKey B (3)'). Daz: .dsf files are DSON (plain UTF-8 JSON, optionally gzipped) storing morph vertex deltas plus formula/ERC links. MetaHuman: the DNA Geometry layer stores 'blend shape target deltas' as sparse (vertexIndices[], deltas[x,y,z]) pairs per blendshape channel per mesh — see the getBlendShapeTargetVertexIndices / getBlendShapeTargetDelta API.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's generator.py hand-codes each face slider as inline arithmetic (`v[idx] += outer(wt*s,[dx,dy,dz])`, plus one-off `v[T.hid(7,0)] += ...` lines and bespoke radial/malar math). That IS a morph target, just fused into code. Factoring it into an explicit D array (K sliders x V verts x 3) makes evaluation a single einsum, makes correctives/combos possible at all, makes sliders serializable and testable, and is the precondition for every other recommendation here.
  - Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EpicGames/MetaHuman-DNA-Calibration/main/docs/dna.md
- **MetaHuman's Rig Logic is a 4-layer pipeline whose Behavior layer does exactly five things — and 'compute corrective expressions' is a first-class stage between controls and geometry.**
  - Epic's own DNA docs list the Behavior layer as: (1) map GUI controls to raw control values, (2) compute corrective expressions, (3) compute joint transformations, (4) compute blend shape channel weights, (5) compute animated map weights. Definition layer holds names of controls/joints/blendshapes/animated maps/meshes, LOD mappings, joint hierarchy, and bind-pose transforms; Geometry layer holds vertices, skin weights and blendshape deltas. Behavior and Geometry are independent so you can load only Behavior to drive a rig. Secondary sources put the MetaHuman face skeleton at roughly 700-800 facial joints (surface joints, volume joints, body-bridge joints) and note that per-vertex displacement shapes exist ONLY at LOD0 while joints carry the shape at lower LODs.
  - *Implication:* Gives MiniMaster a proven staging order to copy verbatim: user sliders -> resolved raw drives -> COMBINATION CORRECTIVES -> joint/armature deltas -> per-vertex deltas -> subdivide. MiniMaster's current `resolve()` does stage 1 only; there is no corrective stage at all, which is why two sliders pushed together (e.g. cheek_depth + cheek_full, or nose_width + nose_length) can only ever add linearly and go off-model.
  - Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EpicGames/MetaHuman-DNA-Calibration/main/docs/dna.md
- **Production facial rigs spend the MAJORITY of their shape budget on combination correctives, not on base shapes — the published ratio is roughly 40 base shapes to 200-400 combos.**
  - Documented figures: a typical blendshape rig has 40+ FACS sliders, and typically another 200-400 'combination' shapes exist purely to fix up the blends between those 40. Gollum (LOTR) used ~950 blendshapes, ~90% of which were combination shapes. The mechanism is stated plainly: 'combination blending multiplies slider weights together to blend in a corrective'; without it, two simultaneously-active shapes produce a 'double transform' / 'going off-model'. Disney's hybrid facial rigging work uses PSD to combine geometric deformers and blendshapes to mitigate the combinatorial explosion.
  - *Implication:* This is the single biggest architectural gap in MiniMaster. 22 facial sliders with zero correctives means the reachable face space is a pure linear subspace — the mathematical reason outputs read as 'the same face with knobs' rather than distinct people. Even ~20 hand-picked pair correctives (w_ab = clamp(w_a)*clamp(w_b)) would more than double the expressive range for a few hundred lines of code.
  - Source: https://disneyanimation.com/publications/a-hybrid-approach-to-facial-rigging/
- **MakeHuman implements 'one slider affects many things' as a MULTIPLICATIVE TENSOR over macro axes — target weight = product of all macro factor values — and the exact code is short enough to port to numpy today.**
  - From makehuman/apps/humanmodifier.py: `result[tpath] = value * reduce(operator.mul, [factors[factor] for factor in tfactors])`. Macro axes and their discrete values are declared in lib/targets.py as: gender[male,female], age[baby,child,young,old], race[caucasian,asian,african], muscle[maxmuscle,averagemuscle,minmuscle], weight[min,average,max], height[min,average,max], breastsize[mincup,averagecup,maxcup], breastfirmness, bodyproportions[uncommon,regular,ideal]. The scalar->factor math (apps/human.py) is: maleVal=g, femaleVal=1-g; maxweightVal=max(0,w*2-1), minweightVal=max(0,1-w*2), averageweightVal=1-(maxweightVal+minweightVal); muscle/height/cup/firmness/proportions identical in form. Age is 4-way piecewise-linear with breakpoints at age=0 (1yr), 0.1875 (10yr), 0.5 (25yr), 1.0 (90yr): babyVal=max(0,1-age*5.333), youngVal=max(0,(age-0.1875)*3.2), childVal=max(0,min(1,5.333*age)-youngVal). Ethnicity is a 3-simplex normalized to sum 1 (default 1/3 each). A target's final weight is the product of every macro factor named in its own filename path.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's `resolve()` fakes this with ~16 hand-tuned linear couplings (`p['shoulders']*(1-0.14*gm)`, `d['bust_drive']=clip(bust+0.8*max(gm,0))`, a 6-entry face dict, etc.) plus a `_headroom_clip` hack. Replacing that with a small macro tensor gives you gender x weight x muscle = 2x3x3 = 18 real shape slots per region instead of one scalar bias, and it composes correctly and monotonically without headroom clipping. It also gives you age and body-proportions for free as new axes.
  - Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/master/makehuman/apps/humanmodifier.py
- **MakeHuman ships 249 modifiers, of which 138 are head/face — I counted them directly from the shipping JSON.**
  - Exact per-group counts from makehuman/data/modifiers/modeling_modifiers.json: head 17, forehead 4, eyebrows 3, neck 8, eyes 34, nose 21, mouth 22, ears 22, chin 7, cheek 8, torso 9, hip 7, stomach 4, buttocks 1, pelvis 2, armslegs 60, breast 8, genitals 3, macrodetails 5, macrodetails-universal 2, macrodetails-height 1, macrodetails-proportions 1. TOTAL 249. Eyes/ears/cheek are duplicated L/R, so mirror-locked the face set is ~106 sliders. The NOSE group alone (21) is: trans down|up, trans backward|forward, trans in|out, scale-vert, scale-horiz, scale-depth, nostrils-width, point-width, base down|up, width1, width2, width3 (three independent widths at three heights!), compression compress|uncompress, curve concave|convex, greek, hump, volume, nostrils-angle, point down|up, septumangle, flaring. CHEEK is: bones, volume, inner, trans down|up (per side). Each modifier is a 2- or 3-target blend: UniversalModifier.getFactors gives left = -min(v,0), center = 1-abs(v), right = max(0,v).
  - *Implication:* This is a ready-made, battle-tested slider taxonomy that directly answers the user's named asks. 'Nose bridge height and length' maps to nose-scale-vert + nose-scale-depth + nose-hump + nose-greek + nose-curve concave|convex + nose-compression. 'Cheekbone depth and where they are' maps to cheek-bones (depth) + cheek-trans down|up (where) + cheek-inner (the buccal hollow that reads as 'facial muscles'). MiniMaster has 5 nose sliders and 3 cheek sliders; MakeHuman has 21 and 8(4 mirrored). The gap is not conceptual, it is inventory.
  - Source: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/makehumancommunity/makehuman/master/makehuman/data/modifiers/modeling_modifiers.json
- **Daz's whole customization system is a driven-property graph (ERC) on top of morph deltas, and its most important shape category — JCM/CBS — is triggered by JOINT ROTATION, not by a user slider.**
  - JCMs (Joint Corrective Morphs; called CBS / Corrective Modifiers on Genesis 9) are morphs driven by joint rotation values, described as an 'if this then that' interpolated on the dialed value — a bicep that flexes when the arm bends 90 degrees. 'ERC Freeze' builds the link so the morph ramps in gradually as the rotation approaches the key value rather than popping; the applied morph is registered as a sub-component of the controller. Genesis 9 exposes a single master dial 'BASE JOINT CORRECTIVES' at 100% that enables all of them. DSON formulas carry a 'stage' field specifying how multiple contributions to the same property combine (sum vs multiply). ERC is also used for one-to-many: one dial curling every joint of a dragon tail.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster does linear blend skinning on the cage over a 16-bone armature with NO pose correctives. Every LBS rig collapses at elbow/knee/shoulder/hip. Adding 6 JCMs (L/R elbow, L/R knee, L/R shoulder-raise), each a delta ramped 0->1 as the joint goes 0->90 degrees, is a few dozen lines and is exactly what Daz ships. It is also the same math shape as the face correctives, so one mechanism buys both.
  - Source: https://www.versluis.com/2021/08/jcms/
- **Daz's HD morphs prove the cage+subdivision architecture MiniMaster already has: broad shape lives on the ~16k-vert base cage, fine detail is a SEPARATE delta layer applied at a specific subdivision level, rendering at ~1M points.**
  - Reported behavior: 'a scene object can have only 16,000 points (vertices) in the DAZ Studio scene but is calculated and displayed as a 1,000,000 points object in the final render window' via OpenSubdiv uniform subdivision. An HD morph sculpted for SubD level 3 only appears when SubD level 3 is dialed in; the ZBrush->Daz HD morph plugin supports a maximum subdivision level of 3. In Blender the HD shape arrives as a multiresolution modifier, explicitly NOT a shapekey. Daz base figure vertex counts (community-reported): Genesis 1 = 19,296 verts / 18,872 polys, Genesis 2 = 21,556 / 21,098, Genesis 3 = 17,418 / 17,000; Genesis 8F ~17,020 and 8M ~16,384 verts. Genesis 9 is a single genderless base.
  - *Implication:* Validates MiniMaster's core choice and tells you where to put detail. Catmull-Clark is LINEAR in cage positions, so the level-N subdivision operator can be precomputed once as a sparse matrix S (V_sub = S @ V_cage) — then HD deltas for nasolabial fold, tear trough, philtrum, nostril crease can be added AFTER subdivision at essentially zero extra cost per frame, without ever densifying the cage. That is how you get 'realistic' without a 5,000-vert cage.
  - Source: https://www.daz3d.com/forums/discussion/180391/guide-working-with-hd-addons-and-subdivision-levels
- **MiniMaster's head cage is topologically incapable of realistic eyes and mouth: an 11x16 lat-long grid has ZERO concentric loops around the orbit or the oral opening, where the documented minimum is 2-3 and the recommended figure is 4-5.**
  - Face-topology guidance is consistent across sources: at least 2-3 concentric loops around the eyes and mouth, with 4-5 recommended for an extended range of expressions; fewer than two produces deformation artifacts on mouth-open or wide smile. The standard build order is: lay the ORBITAL loop around the eyes first, then the ORAL loop around the mouth, then connect them with the nasolabial structure — 'these zones define the entire edge flow of the face'. Loops should be circular around the underlying muscle structures and run parallel/perpendicular to expected deformation.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's head is 11 rings x 16 columns = 178 verts with 9 of 16 columns on the face front, and 'sockets' are just weighted vertex subsets of that grid (T.GROUPS['socket_l'] etc.). No amount of slider tuning fixes this — eye_size currently scales grid verts about a point, which smears the surrounding cheek/brow because there is no loop boundary to contain it. This, not slider count, is the reason the faces do not read as human. Catmull-Clark handles extraordinary vertices and n-gons fine, so the cage does NOT need to stay a lat-long grid.
  - Source: https://vsquad.art/blog/modeling-guide-to-achieving-good-face-topology
- **MetaHuman Creator's realism guarantee comes from constraining the edit space to a database of real scans and blending presets barycentrically — 3 presets on an 'influence circle' — with per-REGION as well as global blending.**
  - Epic's description: create characters 'by selecting a preset as a starting point, blending it with others, and then intuitively sculpting and refining the results ... within the constraints of our database of real-world scans'. Blend tool: up to three presets can be dragged into three slots on an Influence Circle, and a control point moved inside the resulting triangle blends between them. Presets can be blended 'globally, or in local regions', and skeletal proportions vs shaping can be blended independently or together. Extreme changes are blocked to keep results in plausible human ranges.
  - *Implication:* A direct, cheap upgrade path: author K archetype head cages (say 8-12 covering ancestry/sex/age), blend them with barycentric weights over a triangle, and multiply each archetype's contribution by a per-region falloff mask (brow / orbit / nose / midface / mouth / jaw). This gives combinatorially more distinct faces than 22 independent sliders ever can, and the output is automatically plausible because it is a convex combination of plausible inputs — no clamping heuristics needed.
  - Source: https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/metahuman/metahuman-creator-blend-tool-in-unreal-engine
- **SMPL gives the exact, citable decomposition MiniMaster's body should use: T(beta,theta) = T_bar + Bs(beta) + Bp(theta), then skin — i.e. shape deltas AND pose deltas are applied to the rest mesh BEFORE LBS, never after.**
  - SMPL uses 10 shape blend shapes (beta in R^10). M(beta,theta) = W(T(beta,theta), J(beta), theta, W): the blend-skinning function takes the already-morphed rest shape, the SHAPE-DEPENDENT joint locations J(beta), the pose, and the skinning weights. Pose-dependent blend shapes are a linear function of the elements of the pose ROTATION MATRICES (not Euler angles), learned from scans to fix LBS collapse.
  - *Implication:* Two concrete corrections for MiniMaster. (a) Joint locations must be a function of the shape parameters — MiniMaster's joint_layout(d) does derive joints from drives, which is right, so keep that and make it explicit as J(beta). (b) Pose correctives must be keyed on rotation-matrix entries, which is numerically stable and avoids gimbal/wrap problems that an angle-keyed JCM would hit. Both are drop-in for the existing _lbs() path.
  - Source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/2816795.2818013
- **At 28-32mm tabletop scale, essentially every facial slider MiniMaster exposes is BELOW the reliable feature size of resin printing — the realism goal and the print goal need different delta gains.**
  - Cited print limits: minimum wall 0.5-0.8mm for non-structural features, 1.0-1.5mm where handled; walls under 0.8mm are highly prone to deformation in SLA; supported walls minimum ~0.4mm; positive detail (embossed ribs/text) reproducible down to ~0.1-0.2mm only if not isolated and well supported; fins/pins under 0.5mm are fragile. MY DERIVATION (arithmetic, not a citation) at 32mm for a 1.75m human = 1:54.7: head height 23cm -> 4.21mm, head width 15cm -> 2.74mm, nose projection 2cm -> 0.37mm, lip thickness 1cm -> 0.18mm, eye opening 1cm -> 0.18mm, philtrum depth 2mm -> 0.04mm. MiniMaster's own face deltas are ~0.02-0.045 Hh; at a ~4.2mm head that is 0.08-0.19mm of travel — at or below the printable threshold.
  - *Implication:* Two outputs, one model. Add a `detail_gain` multiplier applied to the face delta matrix: 1.0 for screen/render, ~2.5-3.0 for STL export (which is exactly what real mini sculptors do — heroic-scale exaggeration). Without it, sliders that look correct on screen are literally unprintable noise, and the user's 'actual realistic' goal and the 28-32mm target will keep fighting each other invisibly.
  - Source: https://formlabs.com/eu/blog/minimum-wall-thickness-3d-printing/
- **Tabletop mini creators (Hero Forge, Eldritch Foundry) are PARTS-ASSEMBLY systems, not morph systems — which is why they scale to 'billions of combinations' but cannot do continuous facial identity.**
  - Hero Forge: web/WebGL with a custom 3D engine, customizing six primary elements — species, head, body, clothing, gear, base stage — via modular components (extra arms, wings, prosthetics), described as parametric modeling over predefined models. Notably Hero Forge does NOT guarantee home printability: 'There's no promise what you make will be 3D printable ... but only on their 3D printers', with thin free-standing wire parts called out as the failure mode. Eldritch Foundry: pick an ancestry first (which drives overall appearance), then sliders + dropdowns + direct manipulation, hundreds of weapons/clothing options, fully poseable, professional print or STL download.
  - *Implication:* Confirms MiniMaster's morph-based approach is the DIFFERENTIATOR, not a deficit — no mini creator on the market does continuous MetaHuman-style facial identity. It also warns that neither competitor solves watertight-printability by construction; MiniMaster's watertight-STL-by-construction cage is a real advantage worth protecting (do not adopt boolean-union parts assembly, which is what forces those vendors into repair pipelines).
  - Source: https://heroforge.com/content/support-docs/about-hero-forge-our-minis/
- **Reallusion CC4 splits expression morphs into a Standard tier (60+) and Extended tier (140+) organized by anatomical feature — a useful template for tiering MiniMaster's UI without overwhelming users.**
  - CC4 Standard covers 11 facial feature groups: Brow, Eye Blink, Eyelid, Eye Look, Nose, Cheek, Mouth Base, Mouth Blend, Jaw, Tongue, Head. Extended adds 4 more: Eye Lash, Eye Ball (pupil), Neck, Ear. Head and body morph sliders are authored separately (there is a documented workflow for making one slider drive both head and body).
  - *Implication:* Gives a defensible two-tier slider UI: ship a ~20-slider 'Standard' face panel matching today's set, and an 'Extended' panel that exposes the full ~55-106 slider bank. Same underlying delta matrix, no extra runtime cost, and it keeps the app approachable while satisfying the deep-customization ask.
  - Source: https://www.reallusion.com/character-creator/skingen.html

### Recommendations

- [medium/transformative] **Refactor face+body slider math out of generator.py into an explicit named morph-target bank, and evaluate it as one matrix product. This is the enabling change for everything below.** — Build MORPHS: dict[str, tuple[idx: int[], delta: float[n,3]]] at import time by running today's hand-coded atoms once per slider at value +1 and once at -1 against the neutral cage (finite difference on head_verts_local). Store as a sparse (K x V x 3) bank exactly like DNA's getBlendShapeTargetVertexIndices/getBlendShapeTargetDelta pairing. Runtime becomes V = V0 + einsum('k,kvj->vj', w, D). For MiniMaster's 178-vert head and ~60 channels that is a ~32k-float array — trivial. Keep the current procedural code as the AUTHORING tool (tools/ script) that bakes the bank; delete it from the hot path.
- [medium/transformative] **Add a combination-corrective (PSD) stage between resolved drives and vertex deltas, mirroring Rig Logic's 'compute corrective expressions' step.** — COMBOS: list[(slider_a, slider_b, delta_array)] with weight w_ab = clamp(w_a,0,1)*clamp(w_b,0,1) (use clamp(-w,0,1) for the negative lobe). Start with the ~20 pairs that actually double-transform in the current rig: nose_width x nose_length, nose_bridge_height x nose_bridge_depth, cheek_depth x cheek_full, cheek_height x cheek_depth, jaw_width x chin_width, jaw_width x face_width, eye_size x eye_spacing, eye_size x eye_depth, brow_height x brow_depth, lip_full x mouth_width, chin_length x jaw_width, face_length x chin_length, plus gender x each of {jaw_width, brow_depth, cheek_height, lip_full}. - [medium/high] **Replace resolve()'s ~16 hand-tuned linear couplings and _headroom_clip with MakeHuman's multiplicative macro tensor.** — Port verbatim: maleVal=g, femaleVal=1-g; for each of weight/muscle: maxVal=max(0,x*2-1), minVal=max(0,1-x*2), avgVal=1-(maxVal+minVal). Give each body region a macro target set indexed by (gender, weight, muscle) = 2x3x3 = 18 slots and weight each slot by the PRODUCT of its three factor values (humanmodifier.getTargetWeights). Add age as a 4th axis using MakeHuman's exact piecewise-linear breakpoints (age 0=1yr, 0.1875=10yr, 0.5=25yr, 1.0=90yr; babyVal=max(0,1-age*5.333), youngVal=max(0,(age-0.1875)*3.2), childVal=max(0,min(1,5.333*age)-youngVal), oldVal=0 below 0.5). Products are automatically monotone and stay in [0,1], so _headroom_clip and the 0.25*span fudge can be deleted.
- [large/transformative] **Retopologize the head cage with true orbital and oral loops. This is the highest-impact change for 'actual realistic' and no slider work substitutes for it.** — Break the 11x16 lat-long grid. Target layout: keep a skull/cranium grid for rings 8-10 (forehead, crown) but replace rings 3-8 on the face front with (a) an ORBITAL patch per eye of 2 concentric 12-vert loops (24 verts/eye, 48 total) with an eyelid split, (b) an ORAL patch of 2 concentric 16-vert loops (32 verts) with the vermilion border as its own loop, (c) a nose patch carrying bridge/dorsum/wings/columella/nostril rim as ~30 verts. Documented minimum is 2-3 concentric loops around eye and mouth; 4-5 for expression range — build 2 now, leave the ring-insertion hook for 4. Cage budget: grow the head from 178 verts / 176 quads to ~450-650 verts / ~500-700 quads. Subdivision fanout is x4 per level, so at L2 that is ~8-11k quads and at L3 ~32-45k quads — still numpy-fast, and comparable to Daz's 16k-vert base -> ~1M render points pattern.
- [medium/high] **Add the three missing features the current spec explicitly omits and that most control whether a face reads as human: eyelids, nostrils, and a separate lip (vermilion) loop.** — They fall out of the retopology above at near-zero extra cost: eyelid = the inner orbital loop split into upper/lower with a lash-line crease vertex row; nostril = 6-8 verts forming a rim loop on the underside of each nose wing plus one recessed vertex; vermilion = one extra 16-vert loop between the current mouth-line ring and the lip rings, offset ~0.004 Hh outward. - [medium/high] **Precompute the Catmull-Clark subdivision operator as a sparse matrix and add an HD-delta layer applied AFTER subdivision, following Daz's HD-morph pattern.** — Subdivision is linear in cage positions, so build S_L (sparse, shape (V_sub x V_cage)) once per level in core/subdiv.py; then V_sub = S_L @ V_cage. ## blender-body-modeling — how human bodies/heads are actually modeled in Blender (box modeling + subsurf, sculpt→retopo, skin-modifier blockout), and what that implies for MiniMaster's quad cage, slider system, and loop layout

### Findings

- **Real face topology is NOT a latitude/longitude ring grid — it is two concentric loop systems (orbital + oral) joined by a nasolabial bridge. This is the single largest structural gap in MiniMaster's head cage.**
  - The canonical retopo order every guide states: lay the orbital loop around the eye first, then the oral loop around the mouth, then connect them with the nasolabial structure, then build everything else outward. The loops mirror the two circular muscles: orbicularis oculi and orbicularis oris. Recommended density for animation: 3-4 concentric loops around the eye orbit (reducible to 2 for low-poly/mobile), and 2-3 concentric loops around the lips PLUS a separate loop along the very lip edge. Additional loops radiate from the nostrils, and edge rings ring the chin and neck. Loops run from the eye socket up over the brow, down across the cheek to the mouth corner, and back along the jawline to the ear — this forms the 'mask' of the face.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's head is 11 lat/long rings x 16 columns with no eye or mouth loop. That topology structurally CANNOT express eyelid rims, lip rolls, nasolabial folds, or 'cheekbone depth affecting facial muscles' — the vertices don't follow the muscles, so any slider that pushes them produces a bulge on a sphere rather than an anatomical shift. Every facial slider the user named (nose bridge height/length, cheekbone depth+position) is a loop-ring displacement in real practice.
  - Source: https://vsquad.art/blog/modeling-guide-to-achieving-good-face-topology
- **Blender Studio's own realistic-human research sculpts variation as SHAPE KEYS on a base mesh at 2-3 subdivision levels, reserving higher levels for tertiary detail only.**
  - From the 'Shapes Approach' / 'Creating Facial Shapes' chapters of Blender Studio's Realistic Character Workflow (Project Heist): they sculpt shape keys instead of sculpt layers so they 'ignore details and focus on general volumes and shifting of the surface first'; shapes were sculpted at 2-3 subdivisions, with anything above reserved for pores and wrinkles. Sculpted shapes are then applied as corrective shape keys driven by bone positions to integrate exact shapes into the rig. Order of forms: primary masses first, then secondary forms (muscle groups, fat pads, bridge of nose, lip shape).
  - *Implication:* This validates a cage+subdivision architecture, but says the deltas should live at subdiv level 2-3 (a few thousand verts), not on the 403-vert cage. It also validates driving shapes from bone state — i.e. 'cheekbone depth affects facial muscles' is implemented as a corrective/combination shape, not as a formula.
  - Source: https://studio.blender.org/training/realistic-human-research/the-shapes-approach/
- **Blender Studio's base-mesh spec: quad-only, even density, as FEW poles as possible, and deliberately mid-density — not too high (so you can subdivide), not too low (so you can reshape proportions).**
  - From 'Use of Base Meshes': 'relatively even, quad-only topology with as few poles as possible (vertices with more or less than 4 connected edges)'; the topology 'does not need to be optimised for rigging & animation, even if it can be used for that'. Polycount's BaseMesh wiki adds: aim for ~100% quads, avoid poles of 5+ edges running into each other, keep density fairly uniform, but add more where detail is needed (eyes, ears, nose) so you don't have to over-subdivide just to reach those areas.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's cage is already all-quad, which is right. But 'add density where detail is needed (eyes, ears, nose)' is exactly the opposite of a uniform 16-column head — the current cage forces global subdivision to get face resolution, which is why the face reads as smooth putty.
  - Source: https://studio.blender.org/training/realistic-human-research/use-of-base-meshes/
- **Blender Studio's Human Base Meshes bundle is CC0 and contains 17 meshes including realistic AND stylized male/female bodies plus separate heads, jaws, hands, feet and eyeballs.**
  - Released alongside Blender 3.6 (June 2023), .blend for Blender 3.2+, Creative Commons CC0. 17 assets: complete male and female figures in both stylised and photorealistic variants, plus individual body parts (feet, hands, heads, jaws, eyeballs). Explicitly built 'ready to use with the Multiresolution modifier'. Hosted at download.blender.org/demo/asset-bundles/human-base-meshes/ and mirrored on archive.org as 'Human Base Meshes Bundle v1.0.0'.
  - *Implication:* CC0 means MiniMaster can legally bake these into numpy arrays with no attribution obligation and no runtime Blender dependency. A one-time offline import → quad cage extraction → .npz gives a production-grade anatomically-correct cage (including a real head with eye/mouth loops and a real jaw) for a day of work, versus months of hand-authoring rings. Note: I could not verify vertex/poly counts for these meshes — every host 403'd.
  - Source: https://www.cgchannel.com/2023/06/download-blender-studios-free-human-base-meshes/
- **The 8-head canon gives hard, encodable landmark ratios that a proportion system can enforce as constraints.**
  - 8-head 'heroic/ideal' figure: total width ~2⅓ heads; shoulders = 2 x head width; crotch at exactly 4 heads down (the halfway point of total height); ribcage/upper torso = 2 heads tall, 1¾ heads wide, 1⅓ heads deep; pelvis/lower torso = 1 head tall, 1¼ heads wide, 1 head deep. Nipple line falls near the 2-head line. Elbows align with the navel/natural waist; wrist falls at the greater trochanter (roughly crotch level); relaxed fingertips reach mid-thigh; knees are ~2 heads up from the ground (~6 heads down from the top). Gender: males broader shoulders vs hips, longer torso relative to legs; females wider hips relative to shoulders, shorter torso relative to legs, lower waist position.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's height/arm_len/leg_len/torso_len sliders are currently independent scalars. Encoding these as anchored constraints (crotch=50% height, elbow=navel, wrist=trochanter, shoulders=2 head widths) means sliders stay anatomically plausible at extremes instead of producing gibbon arms — which is the difference between 'customizable' and 'realistic'.
  - Source: https://www.thedrawingsource.com/figure-drawing-proportions.html
- **Head/face canon gives equally hard anchors for the 22 facial sliders.**
  - Eyes sit halfway between top of skull and chin. Face is ~5 eye-widths across. Ear top aligns with the brow, ear bottom with the base of the nose. Mouth sits ~1/3 of the way down from nose base to chin (not halfway). The face divides into three roughly equal vertical thirds: hairline→brow, brow→nose base, nose base→chin.
  - *Implication:* Facial sliders should be defined as signed offsets from these canonical anchors (e.g. eye_height = ±x% of head height from the 50% line), so the neutral value of every slider is by construction an anatomically correct face. Right now the ring positions are hand-placed constants.
  - Source: https://fiveable.me/drawing-foundations/unit-10/proportions-human-body/study-guide/bVYcw96jRRpCUZJk
- **Body deformation topology has three named, specific requirements MiniMaster's cage doesn't meet: shoulder x-loops, armpit density, and asymmetric joint loops.**
  - Shoulder: edge loops from the shoulder should flow down onto the chest and around the back; to define the shoulder you use 'x-loops' — two loops running in opposite directions forming an X — which both define the deltoid and act as the attachment of the arm to the body geometry. Radial loops around the deltoid follow shoulder rotation; edges should spiral with the deltoid, not simply circle the arm. Armpit: 'if you don't have enough geometry in the armpit it will stretch when the arms are above the head; too much and it deforms unnaturally when arms are down' — armpits have high range of motion not visible in T/A-pose, so they need extra loops. Joints: place MORE loops on the compression side (inner elbow, back of knee) and fewer on the stretch side. A loop running directly over a joint line creates a sharp crease and needs supporting loops on either side.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster bridges arms to the torso with a 2x2 armhole patch and has one ring per joint (knee, elbow) with no support loops and no compression asymmetry. Under LBS this will pinch at the elbow/knee and tear at the armpit — the classic symptoms the user would read as 'not realistic'.
  - Source: https://wiki.reallusion.com/Content_Dev:CC_Body_Topology_Guide
- **Cylinder/limb ring counts should be multiples of 4 (8, 12, 16, 20, 24…) to get clean central vertical AND horizontal loops; 8 or 12 are the standard low-poly limb counts.**
  - CGTyphoon's cylinder-cap article: 'Using 8, 12, 16, 20, 24, 28, 32, 36… edges (increasing in increments of +4) will provide the perfect central vertical and horizontal loops in the cylinder cap.' For low-poly cylinders 8 or 12 edges are the most popular counts.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's 8-vert arm and leg tubes and 16-column torso are all multiples of 4 — this is correct and should be preserved. But 8 columns is the floor: legs (thigh/calf) carry more silhouette information than arms and warrant 12, which also gives clean quarter loops for the knee's inner/outer support loops.
  - Source: https://cgtyphoon.com/topology/creating-cylinder-cap-central-loops/
- **MakeHuman — the closest existing parametric-human precedent — uses 13,380 body vertices and implements every slider as a SPARSE per-vertex offset table ('target'), with composite sliders as weighted blends of a few primary targets.**
  - Base mesh vertices 0–13379 are the body; 13380–19157 are helper geometry (for clothes fitting, bone placement etc.). A target is 'a table of changed vertices… an ASCII file containing 3 values that describe how each vertex should be moved' — i.e. sparse (vertex_index, dx, dy, dz) deltas, not formulas. Sliders like height/weight/gender/muscularity are composites; e.g. from four primary age targets (baby, teen, young, old) all intermediate shapes are obtained by blending. A nose-size target simply says the nose-tip vertices move a fraction of a unit in Y.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's 38 sliders are analytic vertex math with a gender slider that re-weights ~16 couplings by hand. The industry-proven alternative is: sculpt/derive N extreme variants once, store sparse deltas as .npz, and evaluate sliders as a weighted sum of delta arrays. It is faster at runtime, arbitrarily more anatomical, and makes 'deep customization' a data problem rather than a code problem.
  - Source: https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/creatingassets/maketarget/targets.html
- **Standard Blender box-modeling step order for a body is: torso cube → loop cut + mirror(clipping) → extrude head & legs → loops at leg bottoms → extrude feet → loop around torso → extrude arms → hands modeled separately → join + Merge by Distance.**
  - Consolidated from the Blender wikibook and multiple tutorial writeups: start from a centered cube, add a loop cut, add Mirror with Clipping ON, refine the torso with proportional editing, extrude the head and legs, add loops around the head and at the bottoms of the legs, extrude the head and feet, then add a loop around the torso to extrude the arms. Hands/feet are done as separate blocks (extrude fingers/toes) then Ctrl+J joined and merged by distance. Modifier stack order matters: Mirror must be ABOVE Subdivision Surface — reversed, the merge happens after smoothing and you get sharp seams and shading errors at the midline. Edge creases (Shift+E) are used on specific transitions such as jaw-to-neck.
  - *Implication:* Two operational takeaways: (1) MiniMaster should build/store only the +X half of the cage and mirror-with-clip at generation time — halves the authoring surface and guarantees a watertight, artifact-free midline; (2) MiniMaster's Catmull-Clark implementation should support per-edge crease weights, which artists rely on for jaw/neck, lip line, and eyelid rim. Standard Catmull-Clark alone cannot hold those.
  - Source: https://en.wikibooks.org/wiki/Blender_3D:_Noob_to_Pro/Modeling_a_Human_Character_-_Modeling
- **The skin-modifier blockout path — vertices+edges with a per-vertex radius, auto-generating a skinned surface and an armature — is the closest existing analogue to a procedural generator and is explicitly a base-mesh-for-sculpting tool, not a final topology.**
  - Blender manual: the Skin modifier 'uses vertices and edges to create a skinned surface, using a per-vertex radius' and is 'a quick way to generate base meshes for sculpting and/or smooth organic shapes'. Ctrl+A drags per-vertex thickness; vertices can be marked Root, which becomes the origin for root bones; there is a 'Create Armature' button that generates a matching armature. It cannot be used in sculpt mode and must be applied before sculpting or multires; an Armature modifier must sit BELOW Skin so it deforms output rather than changing generated topology. Joey Carlino's 'Make Characters Fast With the Skin Modifier' covers exactly this: hands, adding mass, making muscles, then armature and posing.
  - *Implication:* Confirms MiniMaster's cage-then-subdivide-then-skin ordering is the right pipeline shape (deform after generation, never before). It also suggests a cheap upgrade: a skeleton-plus-radius intermediate representation would let proportion sliders move joints and radii, with the quad cage regenerated from that — decoupling proportions from topology.
  - Source: https://docs.blender.org/manual/en/latest/modeling/modifiers/generate/skin.html
- **The sculpt→retopo path has a specific, numeric discipline: start with very large voxel size and never decrease it by more than 1.5x per step; switch to Multires once big shapes are locked; voxel remesh is explicitly NOT valid topology for an animated character; dyntopo is discouraged.**
  - From the widely-cited remesh+multires writeup and the Blender manual's retopology page: 'start your sculpt using remeshing with a very large voxel size, decreasing it only when shapes at the current resolution are done'; 'don't decrease voxel size by more than 1.5x at a time, or you will get faceting'; 'the Voxel remesher should not be used for creating topology for a character that will be animated, as such topology has to follow the flow of the geometry and no perfect automatic tools exist for this'; 'don't use dyntopo at all unless you know what you are doing — it won't give smooth surfaces and the subdivision modifier doesn't work well with it'. QuadriFlow aligns edge loops with mesh features and is the better automatic option, but still not animation-grade.
  - *Implication:* Important negative result for MiniMaster: there is no legitimate 'just remesh it' shortcut to a deformable realistic human. The hand-authored (or CC0-derived) quad cage IS the correct architecture — the fix is to improve the cage's loop layout, not to replace it with an automatic remesher.
  - Source: https://medium.com/@skarkkai/remesh-multires-workflow-in-blender-2ae97ae5176d
- **Male/female skull differences reduce to ~6 concrete, sliderable landmark deltas.**
  - Male: larger, more pronounced brow ridges (supraorbital ridges) and a more pronounced, rounded glabella; more sloping forehead; more angular/squarer overall cranium; squarer, more acute gonial (jaw) angle; larger more robust mandible with a squarer, more prominent chin; thicker bone and more defined muscle-attachment areas. Female: smoother, rounder cranium; more upright forehead; smaller jaw with more sloping/obtuse mandibular angle; smaller, more heart-shaped chin, slightly set back.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's gender slider is 'a composite re-weighting of ~16 couplings' — these six give it a defensible anatomical basis for the head specifically: brow_projection, glabella_roundness, forehead_slope_angle, gonial_angle, chin_width, chin_projection. Each is a named landmark the user can also be given as an independent slider.
  - Source: https://dhuamani3d.com/differences-between-male-and-female-skull/
- **Cheekbone sliders need to move a landmark that MiniMaster's cage does not currently have: the malar eminence, which is the point where the head's front plane turns into the side plane.**
  - The malar eminence is the convex bony prominence of the upper cheek where the zygomatic bone's processes converge — 'the visible bump you can feel when you press firmly against the highest part of your cheekbone'. In the Loomis method, 'the side plane of the head starts just where the cheekbone turns away.' The zygomatic arch runs back from it toward the ear. Its shape, height and projection are described as essential to whether a face reads as youthful, aged, masculine, feminine, or ethnically distinct.
  - *Implication:* The user explicitly asked for 'depth of cheekbones and where they are'. That requires (a) a discrete malar vertex/loop the slider can target, (b) the front/side plane break as an actual edge in the cage, and (c) coupling so raising the malar also lifts the cheek mass and deepens the sub-zygomatic hollow — that coupling IS 'affecting facial muscles' in slider terms.
  - Source: https://www.rensoart.com/the-zygomatic-bone-how-the-cheekbone-shapes-light-form-and-expression-in-portrait-painting/
- **Hand topology has a small, known recipe — mitten hands can become real hands with modest cage cost.**
  - Budget loops asymmetrically: more loops at the primary knuckles, fewer in the finger segments and palm center. All-quad around every deformation area — 'a five-sided polygon on a knuckle will almost always distort badly'. A loop running directly over a joint line creates a sharp crease, so add supporting loops either side. Each finger has three bending points; the thumb adds a rotational joint at its base. The palm needs an extra loop that wraps around to the BACK of the hand to support thumb swivel and contain its deformation.
  - *Implication:* Direct spec for replacing MiniMaster's mitten: 5 tubes off a palm patch, 3 joint loops + supports per finger, one wrap-around palm/back-of-hand loop for the thumb. At 28mm print scale fingers are ~0.4mm wide, so this matters more for the render target than the print target.
  - Source: https://www.tripo3d.ai/blog/explore/smart-mesh-topology-for-hands-fingers-low-poly
- **At 28-32mm print scale, facial slider deltas below roughly 0.05-0.1mm are physically unprintable — the print target and the 'realistic render' target need different detail budgets.**
  - For 28mm heroic-scale figures, 'the difference between a readable face and a featureless blob is about 20 microns of XY detail'; recommended XY resolution at or below 25µm, with 17-22µm giving clean facial features without post-processing; minimum reproducible feature size cited as under 100µm. SLA/DLP achieve 25-50µm XY; FDM maxes out near 400µm (nozzle-limited) and cannot do miniature faces. Arithmetic on the 8-head canon: a 30mm figure has a ~3.75mm head, so the nose is roughly 0.6-0.7mm long and an eye ~0.5mm wide.
  - *Implication:* Concretely: a cheekbone-depth slider whose full range is ±0.3mm on a 30mm figure spans only ~12 print-resolution steps — usable but coarse; a nose-bridge-height slider at ±0.15mm is at the edge. Recommend exporting at a user-chosen scale (28/32/54/75mm) and/or exaggerating facial relief at small scales, since realistic-magnitude facial variation vanishes at 28mm.
  - Source: https://store.heygears.com/blogs/blog/warhammer-miniatures-how-to-choose-the-best-resin-3d-printer-for-28mm-models
- **A-pose is the better rest pose for shoulder/armpit deformation; T-pose is easier to author and rig.**
  - A-pose 'behaves better around the shoulders in many pipelines' and gives 'more natural shoulder deformation and less distortion in the armpit area'; T-pose is axis-aligned, more intuitive for modeling, and what most auto-riggers expect. A common compromise is to model in T-pose and deliver in A-pose for rigging.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster generates the cage analytically, so it can do the hybrid for free: author ring positions in T-pose math, then apply a fixed ~40-45° shoulder rotation to the cage before binding. That single change materially reduces armpit tearing for posed miniatures.
  - Source: https://polycount.com/discussion/192810/t-pose-vs-a-pose

### Recommendations

- [large/transformative] **Rebuild the head cage around orbital and oral loop systems instead of 11 lat/long rings. This is the highest-leverage change in the whole project and is the prerequisite for every facial slider the user named.** — Target layout: (1) eye mask — an 8-vert eyelid rim loop per eye plus 2 more concentric loops (3 rings total, the animation minimum is 3-4; 2 is the low-poly floor), with the eyeball socket recessed behind the rim; (2) mouth — a 12-vert lip-edge loop plus 2 concentric loops (3 rings); (3) nose — its own radial patch with an ala loop that starts at the top of the nose, runs along the ala, and terminates where the philtrum meets the nostrils; (4) a nasolabial chain connecting the ala to the mouth corner; (5) a jaw/chin edge ring and a neck ring for head rotation; (6) the remaining skull as a 16-column shell as today, with 5-poles placed on the flat temple/upper-forehead area, never on the eyelid, lip, or jaw. Expect roughly 600-900 cage quads for the head vs today's 176.
- [medium/transformative] **Bake Blender Studio's CC0 Human Base Meshes into .npz as the shipped base cage rather than hand-authoring rings — one-time offline Blender step, zero runtime dependency, zero attribution obligation.** — Download human-base-meshes bundle v1.0.0 (17 meshes: realistic + stylized male/female bodies, plus separate head, jaw, hands, feet, eyeball meshes; .blend for Blender 3.2+; CC0) from download.blender.org/demo/asset-bundles/human-base-meshes/ or the archive.org mirror. In Blender: append the realistic male and female bodies + head, verify all-quad, export OBJ, then convert to numpy (verts float32 array, quads int32 array) and commit as a data file. Use the male and female meshes as the two poles of the gender axis rather than 16 hand-tuned couplings. Caveat: I could not verify their exact vertex counts — check on import and, if they are denser than your cage budget, decimate with a manual quad retopo pass rather than a voxel remesh.
- [large/transformative] **Convert the 38 sliders from analytic vertex math to sparse morph-target deltas summed at evaluation time — the MakeHuman architecture.** — Storage format per slider: an int32 index array plus a float32 (N,3) delta array, i.e. only the vertices that actually move. Evaluate as verts = base + sum(w_i * delta_i) with scatter-add. Composite sliders (gender, weight, muscle) become weighted blends of a handful of primary targets, exactly as MakeHuman derives all ages from 4 primary age targets. This makes 'deep customization' a data-authoring problem: each new slider is a sculpted variant exported to .npz, not new code. Apply deltas at subdivision level 0 (the cage) for proportion sliders and at level 2 for facial sliders, matching Blender Studio's practice of sculpting shapes at 2-3 subdivisions.
- [medium/high] **Add per-edge crease weights to the Catmull-Clark implementation and tag the anatomical crease set.** — Catmull-Clark with sharp/crease weights: at each subdivision step, an edge with crease weight w > 0 uses the crease midpoint rule (or blends between smooth and sharp by w), and its endpoints use the crease vertex rule; decrement w by 1 each level. Tag with weight ~0.5-1.0: eyelid rim, lip line (inner lip edge), nasolabial chain, alar crease, jaw-to-neck transition (the exact edge artists crease with Shift+E in Blender), and the nail/knuckle lines if fingers are added. Without this, subdivision washes out every crease and the face reads as putty regardless of cage quality.
- [medium/high] **Fix the body cage's deformation topology: shoulder x-loops, armpit density, and asymmetric joint loop triads.** — (a) Replace the flat 2x2 armhole patch with x-loops — two loops crossing in opposite directions over the deltoid — with the shoulder loop continuing down onto the pectoral and around the back toward the lats. (b) Add 2 extra rings in the armpit region (the current single armpit ring will tear when the arm goes overhead). (c) At elbow and knee, use a 3-loop triad: one loop on the joint line plus a support loop each side, with the two support loops packed tighter on the compression side (inner elbow, back of knee) and spread on the stretch side. (d) Bump thigh/mid-thigh/knee/calf/shin from 8 to 12 columns — still a multiple of 4, so central vertical and horizontal loops stay clean.
- [small/high] **Encode the 8-head canon as proportion CONSTRAINTS that sliders offset from, rather than as free scalars.** — Anchor rules to enforce after slider evaluation: crotch ring at exactly 50% of total height; shoulder ring width = 2.0 x head width; overall silhouette width ~2.33 head widths; ribcage block = 2 heads tall x 1.75 heads wide x 1.33 heads deep; pelvis block = 1 head tall x 1.25 heads wide x 1.0 head deep; nipple/chest ring at the 2-head line; elbow ring at the navel line; wrist ring at the greater-trochanter/crotch line; relaxed fingertips at mid-thigh; knee ring 2 heads above the ground. Let the height slider select a head-count between ~7.0 (stocky/realistic) and 8.0 (heroic) and rescale all anchors, so extreme slider values stay plausible instead of producing gibbon arms.
- [small/high] **Re-express the 22 facial sliders as signed offsets from canonical facial anchors so that all-zero equals a correct face by construction.** — Anchors: eye line at 50% of skull-top-to-chin; interocular spacing such that face width = 5 eye widths (eye_spacing slider offsets from this); ear top at the brow line, ear bottom at the nose base (ear_size scales about that span); mouth line at 1/3 of the distance from nose base to chin; the three equal thirds hairline→brow, brow→nose base, nose base→chin (face_length redistributes these). Define every slider as a percentage of head height/width rather than absolute units so head_size scaling stays coherent.
- [medium/high] **Add an explicit malar eminence landmark plus the front/side plane break, and couple the cheek sliders to it.** — Introduce a malar vertex (or short 3-vert loop) per side positioned below the outer eye corner, and make the front-plane/side-plane transition an actual cage edge running from the malar back toward the ear along the zygomatic arch. Then: cheek_height slides the malar along the arch; cheek_depth pushes it along the face normal; cheek_full inflates the mass below it. Couple them — raising/projecting the malar should also deepen the sub-zygomatic hollow beneath it and lift the nasolabial chain's upper end. That coupling is the concrete implementation of the user's 'affecting facial muscles'.
- [small/medium] **Author only the +X half of the cage and mirror-with-clipping at generation time, in that stack order (mirror, then subdivide).** — Store half the cage; at build time reflect across X, weld vertices whose |x| < epsilon onto the midline (Blender's Clipping equivalent), THEN run Catmull-Clark. Blender artists are explicit that reversing this order — smoothing before the merge — produces sharp seams and shading errors at the midline. Halves the authoring/delta surface and guarantees a watertight midline for STL export. Optionally add a small asymmetry pass afterward, since Blender Studio calls asymmetry 'a key element that can sell or break the realism of a character's face'.
- [medium/medium] **Replace mitten hands with 5-tube hands using the standard minimal quad recipe.** — Palm patch with 5 tube attachments; each finger an 8-sided tube with 3 joint loops (matching the three bending points) plus a support loop on each side of every knuckle loop, denser at the primary (base) knuckle and sparse mid-segment; thumb attached at a rotational base; one extra palm loop that wraps around onto the BACK of the hand to contain thumb swivel. Keep all-quad through every knuckle — a 5-sided face on a knuckle distorts badly. Gate this behind a detail level: at 28mm a finger is ~0.4mm wide, near the printable floor.
- [small/medium] **Rotate the cage into an A-pose (~40-45° at the shoulder) before armature binding, while keeping the ring math in T-pose.** — Author ring centers/radii in T-pose (axis-aligned, easy math), then apply a fixed shoulder rotation to the arm rings before LBS binding. This is the industry hybrid — model in T, deliver in A — and directly reduces the armpit stretching and shoulder pinching that flat 2x2 armhole patches produce.
- [small/medium] **Report print-scale limits in the UI and offer multiple export scales, since realistic facial variation is sub-resolution at 28mm.** — On an 8-head 30mm figure the head is ~3.75mm, the nose ~0.6-0.7mm long, an eye ~0.5mm wide. Resin printers resolve 25-50µm XY (17-22µm needed for clean faces without post-processing); minimum reliable feature ~100µm. So: offer 28/32/54/75mm export scales; warn when a facial slider's displacement at the chosen scale falls below ~0.05mm; optionally apply a scale-dependent relief exaggeration factor to facial deltas at 28-32mm so the face still reads. I could not verify a specific minimum wall thickness figure — treat any wall-thickness rule as unverified.

## Face and body topology standards for subdivision-surface characters — canonical edge-loop layout, pole legality, and vertex/poly budgets for a realistic head/body control cage, benchmarked against MiniMaster's current 178-vert head and 403-vert body cages.

### Findings

- **A film-grade realistic head control cage is roughly 4,000 control points — MiniMaster's head cage is 178 verts, ~23x smaller.**
  - Pixar's Geri (SIGGRAPH 98, DeRose/Kass/Truong, 'Subdivision Surfaces in Character Animation') used a head control mesh of ~4,100 control points forming ~4,300 polygon faces, subdivided with Catmull-Clark plus semi-sharp creases for eyelids. Note: I could not fetch the PDF (proxy 403); this number came from multiple consistent search-result summaries of that widely-cited paper, so treat it as high-confidence-but-unverified-by-me. Commercial subdivision-ready head base meshes land an order of magnitude lower: one clean quad female head base mesh is 1,192 polygons at cage level and 4,768 after one subdivision; another stylized head base is 1,728 verts / 1,690 faces. MakeHuman's 'Homunculus 08' whole-body base mesh — explicitly described as 'optimized for subdivision surfaces modelling' and 100% quads, zero triangles — is 13,380 body vertices and 14,766 faces (vertex ids 0..13379, continuing to 19157 for helper geometry).
  - *Implication:* Gives us three concrete tiers to aim at. Our 176-quad head at subdiv level 3 produces 11,264 quads of *smooth but featureless* surface; a 1,200-quad feature-driven cage at level 1 produces only 4,800 quads but has actual eyelids, lips and nostril wings. Redistribution beats subdivision. Target: head cage 1,100-1,400 quads, body cage 1,500-2,500 quads, total 2,500-4,000 — 'credible realistic'. MakeHuman parity (~14,700 quads) is the ceiling, Geri (~4,300 for the head alone) is the film reference.
  - Source: https://www.cs.rpi.edu/~cutler/classes/advancedgraphics/S17/papers/derose_subdivision_98.pdf
- **The canonical face is built as two aperture loops (orbital + oral) joined by a nasolabial structure, wrapped by a 'mask' loop — NOT as a stack of horizontal rings.**
  - The standard retopology order is: (1) lay the orbital loop around the eye, (2) lay the oral loop around the mouth, (3) connect them with the nasolabial structure, (4) build everything else off those. The 'mask' of the face is a loop running from the eye socket, up over the brow, down across the cheek to the mouth corner, and back along the jawline to the ear. Supporting rings are then added: loops under the eyes (eye bags), loops radiating from the nostrils, rings around the chin, and neck rings for head rotation. Named requirement: the nasolabial fold must flow from the wings of the nose to the corners of the mouth.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's head is 11 horizontal rings x 16 columns — literally the uniform-grid topology every source names as the failure mode ('uniform density results in too much geometry where it's not needed and too little where it is'; 'a grid-like edge loop around the nose makes the nose look square and big'). No horizontal-ring head can produce a closing eyelid, a vermilion border, or a nasolabial fold, because none of those features are horizontal. This is the single structural reason the current heads cannot read as realistic no matter how many sliders are added.
  - Source: https://vsquad.art/blog/modeling-guide-to-achieving-good-face-topology
- **Concrete loop counts: 3-4 concentric loops around the eye orbit, 2-3 concentric loops around the lips plus a vermilion loop, and a pair of loops for the nostril wing.**
  - Eye: 3-4 circular loops around the orbit is the animation recommendation; the hard minimum is 2 (one along the eyelid margin, one further back for volume and smooth closure), reducible to 2 only at the cost of expression range; the upper eyelid crease is a separate deformation zone that needs its own loop. Mouth: 2-3 concentric loops around the lips plus a separate loop along the very edge (vermilion border); at least one loop must run INSIDE along the lip edge or the geometry visibly collapses when the mouth opens; at least 2 edge loops must exit at the lip corner or the mouth 'looks like it is being cut'. Nose: the alar wings are a separate zone defined by a PAIR of loops, plus a nostril-crease loop whose vertices sit at the deepest, most recessed part of the outer nostril contour. Jaw: at least one loop running chin-to-ear. Ear: a well-structured quad ear is 144 faces with a 12-vertex external boundary loop, with poles at the four corners of that boundary.
  - *Implication:* These are directly implementable as cage-construction parameters. They also give us the per-region quad budget: eye aperture ring of 16 verts x 3 orbital loops = 48 quads/eye plus a 16-quad inner lid-depth ring; mouth aperture of 20 verts x 3 loops = 60 quads plus a 20-quad inner lip ring; nose ~120 quads; ear 144 (or a 40-quad stub at 28 mm print scale). That is ~500 quads of feature geometry, leaving ~700 for cheeks, cranium, jaw and neck to hit a 1,200-quad head.
  - Source: https://wiki.reallusion.com/Content_Dev:CC_Face_Topology_Guide
- **Pole legality has a precise rule: valence 3 (N-pole) and valence 5 (E-pole) only, never 6 or more, and never inside an active deformation zone.**
  - An N-pole is a vertex where 3 edges converge, an E-pole where 5 converge. Under subdivision, 3-sided poles create areas of HIGH vertex density and 5-or-more-sided poles create areas of LOW density; the density mismatch is what causes visible pinching. Best practice is to avoid 6-or-more-sided poles entirely because they 'collapse when deformed and create pinching when subdivided'. A 5-pole at the corner of the mouth is described as inevitable and acceptable — but only if it sits slightly LATERAL of the corner, out of the active deformation zone, where it becomes 'practically invisible during movement'. The general remedy is to move poles toward flat, low-deformation surfaces.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster is currently violating this at the cage level. minimaster/core/subdiv.py loft_rings() caps a ring with N/2 quads fanning to a single pole: for the 16-column head that is a VALENCE-8 pole at the crown and another at the throat, and the ring beneath it alternates valence-3 / valence-5. Valence 8 is well past the 'avoid 6+' threshold and will flatten/pinch the top of the skull under Catmull-Clark. The limb caps in character/topology.py _limb() are fine by contrast — the `for m in range(0, 8, 2)` fan gives a 4-quad, valence-4 cap.
  - Source: https://topologyguides.com/manipulating-edge-poles
- **A bending joint needs exactly two full loops flanking it plus a mid-joint edge that is deliberately NOT a full loop.**
  - The standard: 'on the elbow or knee joint there should be two whole loops which flow around the entire joint', plus 'a set of edges in the middle of the joint (on the back where the elbow is, or the front of the knee) exactly where it will bend' — and those middle edges are explicitly NOT a loop, their flow is cut off so the middle loop does not travel around the whole joint. Sources explicitly warn against three full loops ('you might think three loops would be better... this isn't the case with the joint area'). For hands: 2-3 loops per major knuckle, every finger with the same loop count, and loop counts in multiples of 4 as a general practice. Triangles at joints cut off edge flow and deform badly.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's arm tube gives the elbow exactly ONE ring (shoulder, deltoid, biceps, elbow, forearm, wrist, palm, knuckle) and the leg gives the knee one ring (thigh, mid-thigh, knee, calf, shin). Both joints are one ring short of the minimum and have no mid-joint crease edge — which is why LBS on this cage will pinch/candy-wrapper at the elbow and knee regardless of weight tuning. Fix is cheap: 2 extra rings per elbow, 2 per knee = 4 rings x 8 verts x 2 sides = 64 verts on the body cage.
  - Source: https://polycount.com/discussion/54527/edge-loops-for-elbows-and-knees
- **The shoulder is the one place where concentric rings are wrong — it needs an 'x-loop' / spiral, and the armpit is a named failure zone.**
  - Around the shoulder, edges should SPIRAL with the deltoid's movement rather than simply circle the arm. The named technique is the 'x-loop': two loops going in opposite directions forming an X, which defines the shoulder and provides the attachment of the arm geometry to the body. Shoulder loops should continue down onto the chest and around the back. The armpit/arm socket is called out as a main problem area: insufficient geometry there stretches when the arms go overhead, while too much geometry deforms unnaturally when the arms are down. Deformation-critical zones as a ranked list from Polycount: crotch/hips/butt, shoulders/armpits, mouth corners/cheeks, knees, elbows, hands/fingers.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster currently bridges the arm to the torso with a flat 2x2 armhole patch. That patch has no deltoid loop and no armpit geometry at all, so the shoulder is topologically a hole plugged with a tube — the worst case the sources describe. The x-loop is implementable in the existing cage: continue the deltoid ring down into the chest and back rings instead of terminating it at the armhole rim.
  - Source: http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/ShoulderTopology
- **Standard limb column counts are 12 around the torso / 8 around the arm / 6 for minor parts — MiniMaster's 16/8 is already generous, so the deficit is rings, not columns.**
  - Common practice: a cylinder with 12 divisions around the axis for the torso and 8 for the arm; 8 sides works best for limbs, 6 for very low-poly; subdivisions of 4, 8 or 12 depending on character with smaller preferred; the '12/8/6 plan' makes limb-to-torso merging easy. Also noted: 8-10 faces around the body with 7 vertical internal edges, and 'the more edges you add here, the more edges you need in the legs, arms and neck because they all need to meet edges from the rest of the body'.
  - *Implication:* Confirms we should NOT spend budget widening the torso from 16 columns. Every extra torso column propagates into arms, legs and neck. Spend the budget on (a) joint rings, (b) the head's feature loops, (c) fingers. This also validates the existing 16-column torso / 8-column limb split as a sane skeleton to build on rather than a rewrite.
  - Source: https://www.cs22.space/tutorial-humanoid-model
- **Loops must be diverted under the chin rather than all continuing down the neck, and the head/neck junction needs its own terminator loop.**
  - Not all facial edge loops can continue down the neck — 'there isn't enough room for all that geometry, and if it gets too crowded it causes problems like wrinkling when you smooth the model'; the fix is to divert loops under the chin. The neck loop serves a dual purpose: it terminates the loops coming across the skull, and it defines the neck musculature (sternocleidomastoid, trapezius). Neck topology ranges from a simple cylinder to a reorganized mesh depending on how believable the neck deformation must be. Validation test: open the jaw fully and watch for neck and chin collapse.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's head is a SEPARATE closed shell whose bottom ring is a throat ring capped by a valence-8 pole, floating inside/over the body's neck rings. There is no terminator loop and no shared topology, so head-turn and jaw motion can never be continuous with the neck. Merging the head shell into the body at a shared neck loop (matching vertex counts) is a prerequisite for realistic neck/jaw deformation and also removes an interior pole.
  - Source: http://wiki.polycount.com/wiki/BodyTopology
- **At 28-32 mm print scale, true eyelid and lip geometry IS printable — the resolution floor is ~20-35 microns XY, not a reason to stay featureless.**
  - For crisp tabletop miniatures the target is 20-35 microns XY resolution; for a 28 mm miniature face to have defined eyes and mouth you want 35 um or smaller, with the practical sweet spot 24 um down to 17 um. Past roughly 20 microns 'the resolution race is marketing' — an 8K screen at 22 um and a 16K at 14x19 um give painted 28 mm figures nobody can tell apart. At 0.05 mm layer heights a 28 mm miniature shows every fold and skin crease the model has, and every one it's missing.
  - *Implication:* A 28-32 mm figure has a head roughly 4-4.5 mm tall, so an eye aperture is ~0.4 mm wide — about 17 printer pixels at 24 um. Eyelid thickness, a vermilion border and a nostril crease are all comfortably above the print floor. Pore/wrinkle-level detail is not. So the correct topology target is 'every feature down to ~0.15 mm at print scale' — i.e. lids, lips, alar wings, ear helix — and nothing finer. The print constraint does NOT justify the current featureless head; it only caps us below MetaHuman-class density.
  - Source: https://store.heygears.com/blogs/blog/warhammer-miniatures-how-to-choose-the-best-resin-3d-printer-for-28mm-models
- **MetaHuman-class heads run 7,000-15,000 verts at LOD0 — an order of magnitude above any control cage, because that is a RENDER mesh, not a cage.**
  - MetaHuman head meshes are reported at 7,000-15,000 vertices at LOD0 to support detailed facial movement, with per-vertex Rig Logic deformation existing only at LOD0 (pore-level facial deformation is lost at LOD1). One MetaHuman-compliant custom head is cited at 24,002 polygons / 24,049 vertices. Caveat: these figures came from a third-party Medium pipeline reference and a Fab listing, not from Epic's own documentation — I could not verify them against dev.epicgames.com (proxy blocked).
  - *Implication:* Sets the correct mental model for our pipeline: our CAGE should be compared to Geri (~4,300 faces) and MakeHuman (14,766 faces), and our SUBDIVIDED OUTPUT should be compared to MetaHuman LOD0 (7-15k verts). A 1,200-quad head cage at subdiv level 2 = 19,200 quads, which brackets MetaHuman LOD0 density — so one well-built cage plus level-1 or level-2 subdivision genuinely reaches that fidelity class. We do not need a dense cage; we need a correctly-structured one.
  - Source: https://medium.com/@Jamesroha/metahuman-5-6-5-7-pipeline-reference-170d302b078e

### Recommendations

- [large/transformative] **Replace the 11-ring x 16-column head shell in minimaster/character/topology.py with a feature-driven cage built from aperture loops outward, in the canonical order: orbital loop -> oral loop -> nasolabial bridge -> mask loop -> cranium.** — Target 1,100-1,400 quads / ~1,150-1,450 verts (vs 176/178 today), a 6-8x increase. Budget: eye aperture ring 16 verts (8 upper lid, 8 lower lid) x 3 concentric orbital loops = 48 quads per eye + a 16-quad inner lid-depth ring pushed back onto the eyeball = ~64-130 quads/eye; mouth aperture ring 20 verts (10 upper, 10 lower) x 3 loops (vermilion + 2 orbicularis oris) = 60 quads + 20-quad inner lip ring; nose ~120 quads including a PAIR of alar-wing loops and a nostril-crease loop; nasolabial loop from alar crease to mouth corner; mask loop eye-socket -> brow -> cheek -> mouth corner -> jawline -> ear; one chin-to-ear jaw loop; cranium coarse at ~150 quads (it is the cheapest region). Build this as a new module (e.g. minimaster/character/head_topology.py) with named loop tables so the existing hid(r,c) accessors can be retired region by region.
- [small/high] **Fix the valence-8 crown/throat poles produced by loft_rings() caps, and add a cage-time pole audit as a hard assertion.** — In minimaster/core/subdiv.py, loft_rings() caps an N-ring with N/2 quads fanning to one pole — for N=16 that is a valence-16... no, valence-8 pole (8 cap quads), which exceeds the industry 'never 6 or more' rule and will visibly flatten the crown under Catmull-Clark. Replace with a grid/cross cap that keeps max valence <= 5, or terminate the crown with a 2x2 quad patch. Then add a test in tests/test_subdiv.py or tests/test_character.py that computes the valence histogram of every cage and asserts max(valence) <= 5, plus a whitelist check that no vertex of valence != 4 lies within 1 ring of a declared deformation zone (lid margin, lip line, elbow crease, knee crease, armpit, crotch). The limb caps in _limb() already do this right (range(0, 8, 2) -> 4 quads -> valence 4) — use that as the template.
- [small/high] **Give every bending joint the canonical 2-flanking-loops + non-looping mid-crease-edge layout.** — Elbow currently gets 1 ring, knee gets 1 ring. Add 1 ring above and 1 below each, and add a mid-joint edge set on the outside of the bend (back of elbow, front of knee) that is explicitly NOT a closed loop — its flow terminates so it does not wrap the limb. Cost: elbow 2 rings x 8 verts x 2 arms = 32 verts; knee 2 rings x 8 verts x 2 legs = 32 verts; total 64 verts on a 403-vert cage. Do NOT go to three full loops — sources specifically warn that three full loops at a joint is worse, not better. Same rule for wrist and ankle. Validate by posing to 90 degrees and checking for volume loss before/after.
- [medium/high] **Replace the flat 2x2 armhole patch with a deltoid x-loop that continues into the chest and back rings, and add real armpit geometry.** — The x-loop is two loops crossing in opposite directions over the deltoid. Concretely: run the deltoid ring off the arm tube DOWN into the torso's chest ring on the front and the ribs/scapula ring on the back rather than terminating it at the 2x2 rim, and cross it with a second loop running up over the shoulder from the armpit. Add 2 extra verts at the armpit apex so overhead-arm poses stretch across geometry instead of a single edge. Polycount names the armpit/arm socket as the primary problem area and the x-loop as the named fix. This is the highest-value single change on the body cage.
- [medium/high] **Replace mitten hands with 5 fingers at 6 columns each, 2-3 loops per major knuckle, identical loop count on every finger.** — Per finger: 6 columns around (limbs tolerate 6-8; 6 is the documented low-poly floor), 5 rings (base, MCP knuckle, PIP, DIP, tip) plus a 3-quad valence-3... use a 3-quad cap for a 6-column tube = valence 3, which is legal. That is 30 verts + 1 pole = 31 verts per finger, ~155 verts for four fingers plus a thumb with an extra rotational joint ring at its base (~37 verts). Total ~190 verts added per hand, ~380 for both. Keep loop counts equal across fingers and prefer multiples of 4 for loops per the general practice note. At 28-32 mm scale a finger is ~1.5 mm long and ~0.4 mm thick — above the 20-35 um print floor, so fingers are printable, though they will need a minimum-thickness clamp.
- [medium/high] **Merge the separate head shell into the body at a shared neck terminator loop instead of leaving it a free-floating closed shell.** — Match the head's bottom ring vertex count to the body's neck-top ring (both are currently 16 — this is a free win), weld them, and delete the head's bottom valence-8 pole entirely. Then divert the mask/jaw loops UNDER the chin instead of letting all of them run down the neck (sources: 'there isn't enough room for all that geometry... it causes wrinkling when you smooth the model'). Add one dedicated neck junction loop that terminates the cranial loops and gives the sternocleidomastoid/trapezius somewhere to live. Test: open the jaw fully and check for chin/neck collapse.
- [medium/medium] **Add ears as a 144-quad patch with a 12-vertex boundary loop (or a 40-quad stub at print scale), attached so the four boundary poles land on the flat skull behind the ear.** — The documented clean-ear structure is 144 faces with a 12-vertex external loop specifically designed to attach to a medium/high-poly head. Cut a 12-vertex hole in the temporal region of the new head cage and stitch. Poles from the boundary loop land at the four corners — put them on the flat skull side, never on the helix. If 144 quads x 2 is too much of the head budget, a 40-quad simplified helix/lobe stub still beats the current vertex bumps and preserves the correct 12-vertex attachment interface for a later upgrade.
- [medium/transformative] **Re-express the 22 facial sliders as operations on NAMED LOOPS with falloff measured in graph distance along the cage, not on ring/column indices.** — The user's explicit asks — 'nose bridge height and length', 'depth of cheekbones and where they are, with it affecting facial muscles' — are loop operations, not vertex operations. Define slider targets as loop sets: nose_bridge_height/depth -> the dorsum column between the nose-base and brow loops; cheek_height -> a parameter that SLIDES the cheekbone loop along the mask loop rather than translating verts; cheek_depth -> displacement of that loop with falloff that bleeds into the nasolabial loop and the orbital loop (this is what 'affecting facial muscles' means topologically). Implement falloff as BFS hop-count over the cage's edge graph with a smoothstep, so a change automatically propagates along anatomically-correct paths. This is impossible on the current uniform ring grid and becomes nearly free once the feature-driven cage exists.
- [small/medium] **Reset the subdivision-level policy: build a bigger cage and subdivide LESS.** — Current: 176-quad head at level 3 = 11,264 quads of featureless surface; 401-quad body at level 3 = 25,664 quads. Proposed: ~1,200-quad head + ~2,000-quad body cage, then level 1 for viewport/preview (12,800 quads total) and level 2 for STL export (51,200 quads). Level 2 on a 1,200-quad head gives 19,200 quads, which brackets the reported MetaHuman LOD0 head density (7,000-15,000 verts) — i.e. one correctly-structured cage at level 2 reaches that fidelity class without a dense cage. Level 3 becomes unnecessary and should be dropped from the default path; keep it available but expect diminishing returns.
- [small/medium] **Add a topology conformance test suite encoding the industry rules as assertions.** — Six checks, all cheap on a quad cage: (1) 100% quads, zero triangles (matches MakeHuman's stated 'quads only, completely triangles free'); (2) max vertex valence <= 5, zero valence >= 6; (3) each declared bending joint has exactly 2 flanking full loops (count rings between named ring markers); (4) the mouth-corner 5-pole is >= 1 quad lateral of the aperture corner; (5) closed 2-manifold (already enforced in _cc_once, promote it to an explicit test); (6) region density ratio — face region quads / cranium quads should be >= 4:1, encoding 'the face receives the highest density of geometry, flat non-deforming surfaces receive the minimum'. These turn every finding above into a regression gate.

## realism-cues: geometry vs shading vs texture — what actually makes a CG human read as human rather than a clay mannequin, and the minimum shading/geometry upgrades a pure-numpy software rasterizer can implement this week

### Findings

- **The uncanny valley is triggered by INCONSISTENCY of realism across features, not by low realism per se — so upgrading only the shader while eyes/hands stay mannequin-grade can make MiniMaster look worse, not better.**
  - Experimental work found that 'reducing consistency in human realism increases the uncanny valley effect', while 'increasing category uncertainty does not'. Features at inconsistent levels of realism cause perceptual processes to make conflicting inferences about whether the entity is real, producing large feedback error signals and negative appraisal. The same effect is documented cross-modally (a mismatch in the human realism of face vs voice produces an uncanny valley). Semirealistic film-character studies found the valley appeared only when morphed faces had abnormal features such as bizarre eyes — near-perfect realism is necessary but not sufficient.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster currently pairs a flat-diffuse clay shader with MITTEN hands, no eyelids, no nostrils, no hair. If you bolt on SSS + specular + AO but leave a lidless sphere-in-a-socket eye and a mitten hand, you move features to mismatched realism tiers and land IN the valley. The rule for this week: advance geometry and shading in lockstep, and treat 'no eyelids' as a blocking defect before any photoreal shading work. Alternatively, commit to a consistent semi-stylized tier and win there.
  - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0010027715300755
- **Eyes dominate the realism judgment; a mismatch between eye realism and face realism produces the MOST negative evaluations, and oversized eyes are eeriest on the most realistic faces.**
  - Eye-tracking/category-processing work confirmed the face feature hierarchy eyes > nose > mouth for distinguishing human-like objects. A greater mismatch between the realism of the eyes and the rest of the face elicits more negative evaluations, with the most negative evaluations for fully artificial eyes on a fully realistic face (or vice versa). Unrealistically large eyes appeared the most eerie on the most realistic faces. Practitioner sources put it bluntly: what most often gives away a CG character is 'dead, lifeless eyes', and artists report humans notice a ~1 mm shift in eye shape.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's eye is a plain icosphere with a single flat EYE_COLOR (generator.py:446-454), radius 0.056*head_height, no iris, no pupil, no limbus, no cornea, no lids, no catchlight. That is the single highest-leverage defect in the whole program. It also means the existing `eye_size` slider (±30% radius) is actively dangerous — pushing it positive on an otherwise-realistic head is the exact configuration the literature flags as maximally eerie. Clamp it, and rebuild the eye.
  - Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3590815/
- **Flat diffuse skin reads as plastic/mannequin specifically because light stops at the surface; subsurface scattering is repeatedly described as the single biggest reason a render looks real or fake.**
  - Real skin is a translucent multi-layered material: light penetrates, scatters, and exits elsewhere. Without SSS, light bounces off like a billiard ball, so 'a person's skin reads as a shop mannequin' and results are 'painted plastic regardless of how good the texture is'. Human vision is specifically tuned to skin, so tiny errors register as 'something is off' before they can be named. Real-time practice approximates this with (a) wrap/half-Lambert diffuse, (b) pre-integrated skin shading, (c) baked-thickness translucency.
  - *Implication:* render.py line 150 is literally `ambient + diffuse * clip(N·L, 0, 1)` with a single base color — the exact configuration the sources call 'painted plastic'. This is the cheapest transformative fix available: a per-channel wrapped diffuse plus a curvature term needs no textures, no UVs, and no new passes, and runs entirely in the existing numpy corner-shade code path.
  - Source: https://blog.chaos.com/subsurface-scattering-explained
- **Pre-integrated skin shading turns SSS into a pure per-vertex/per-pixel function of (N·L, surface curvature) with no blur passes — and MiniMaster's Catmull-Clark cage gives it curvature for free.**
  - Eric Penner, SIGGRAPH 2011 'Advances in Real-Time Rendering': pre-blur the diffuse BRDF with a skin diffusion profile and parameterize by curvature; bake into a 2D lookup. Every shaded point is mapped to a sphere of a radius derived from local curvature. Stated goal: 'a simple pixel shader with all inputs stored locally... with no blur passes' and 'no extra rendering passes required'; well suited to deferred renderers, quality is very good.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster already has an exact analytic neighbourhood at cage level (403+178 verts, one-ring adjacency known from the quad topology). Curvature = |Δn|/|Δp| over the one-ring, computed once per slider change on 581 verts, then carried through subdivision as a vertex attribute. This is the technically correct SSS model and it is cheaper here than in a game engine, because the cage IS the curvature parameterization. The LUT can be a 32x32 numpy array generated at import.
  - Source: https://www.scribd.com/presentation/362868115/Penner-Pre-Integrated-Skin-Rendering-Siggraph-2011-Advances-in-Real-Time-Rendering-Course
- **Skin's specular F0 is 0.028 (IOR 1.4) — roughly 6% of incident light reflects directly and uncolored. Skin's realism 'lives more in the specular response than in color'.**
  - For skin PBR the Fresnel reflectance at normal incidence should be F0 = 0.028, derived from an index of refraction of 1.4; roughly 6 percent over the spectrum reflects directly, uncolored, off the rough oily topmost layer. GPU Gems 3 ch.14 uses the Kelemen/Szirmay-Kalos specular BRDF (a Cook-Torrance simplification: Beckmann NDF + Schlick Fresnel + their geometry-term approximation) chosen 'for its efficient evaluation'. Skin texturing practice: the specular effect 'is not uniform and varies depending on location on the body'; oil-rich areas (nose, forehead) read smoother and more reflective; cavity maps break up what would otherwise be 'a consistent sheen'.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster has NO specular term at all, which is why it reads as unfired clay. But the fix is not 'add Phong': a default bright tight Phong lobe on skin reads as wet plastic. The correct look is a DIM (F0=0.028), BROAD (rough), spatially VARYING highlight — and the realism cue is the contrast between matte skin and the tight bright highlight on eyes and lips. Zonal roughness (forehead/nose glossier, cheeks/jaw matter) is implementable as a per-vertex scalar with zero textures.
  - Source: https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-14-advanced-techniques-realistic-real-time-skin
- **Wrap lighting is the documented cheap stand-in for SSS, and the normalized form is known — but the naive version is not energy-conserving and breaks with shadows.**
  - 'An old trick for approximating subsurface scattering is to deliberately modify the N·L part of the lighting calculations.' Half-Lambert = N·L*0.5 + 0.5. Wrapped diffuse = (N·L + a)/(1 + a), with a normalization term reported as (2 + a)/(2*(1 + a)) (Self Shadow, 'Righting Wrap'). Caveat from the same source: common wrap models 'have some deficiencies out of the box — neither is energy conserving and they don't play well with shadows'.
  - *Implication:* Directly replaceable for render.py:150. Doing it PER CHANNEL is what sells skin: red light penetrates skin far deeper than green/blue, so the terminator should shift red before it goes dark. A single scalar wrap only softens; a per-channel wrap produces the characteristic red terminator that separates skin from wax.
  - Source: https://blog.selfshadow.com/2011/12/31/righting-wrap-part-1/
- **Baked-thickness translucency (the Barré-Brisebois/Bouchard 'fast SSS' formula) is implementable with MiniMaster's EXISTING ray caster and gives glowing ears/nostrils/fingers — a strong, cheap realism tell.**
  - The technique (popularized for Unity URP and Frostbite) computes a light vector distorted by the normal, then a back-facing view-dependent term scaled by a pre-baked thickness map: LTLight = normalize(L + N*distortion); LTDot = pow(saturate(dot(V, -LTLight)), power) * scale; LT = attenuation * (LTDot + ambient) * thickness. Thickness is baked by casting rays inward along -N and recording distance to the backface.
  - *Implication:* minimaster/core/raycast.py already implements vectorized Möller-Trumbore. Adding a batched any-hit variant gives you BOTH the AO bake and the thickness bake from one piece of code. On a closed watertight cage this is ~20 lines. Ear rims, nostril wings, and the webbing between fingers lighting up red against a rim light is one of the most instantly 'alive' cues available, and it costs one float per cage vertex.
  - Source: https://johnaustin.io/articles/2020/fast-subsurface-scattering-for-the-unity-urp
- **A realistic eye is a LAYERED assembly — eyeball + cornea shell + tear line/meniscus + occlusion mesh — and each layer has a named job. The occlusion and tearline layers are what kill 'dead eyes'.**
  - Unity HDRP's dedicated Eye Shader exists because eyes 'exhibit important phenomena like cornea refraction, caustics, pupil dilation, limbal darkening, and subsurface scattering'; the limbus/limbal ring is the darkened bordering region between cornea and sclera. MetaHuman/Character Creator ship separate meshes: 'Eye Occlusion simulates the shadows cast from the eyelids' and 'the Tear Line emulates the reflections of the tears at the junction between the eyeballs and the eyelids'; the tearline mesh simulates moisture and tear reflectivity at the tear ducts. SIGGRAPH 2007 introduced a refraction function accounting for refraction at the corneal interface (which makes the pupil appear larger than it is). Artist accounts stress the soft transition where sclera meets lids — partly AO/shadow from brow and lids, partly reflection of the upper lashes — plus 'perfectly placed catchlights'.
  - *Implication:* You can get 80% of this with zero new render machinery: procedural per-vertex color on the existing icosphere (sclera/limbus/iris/pupil by polar angle from the gaze axis), a slight cornea bulge on the front cap, a tight low-roughness specular lobe for the catchlight, and a baked contact-shadow darkening on the upper sclera. The 'occlusion' and 'tearline' layers are geometry MiniMaster does not have yet — but the shadow half of eye occlusion falls out of the AO bake for free once eyelids exist.
  - Source: https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.high-definition@14.0/manual/eye-shader.html
- **AO measurably raises PERCEIVED realism even when physically inaccurate — and in complex scenes, low-accuracy AO is indistinguishable from high-accuracy AO.**
  - A psychophysical experiment comparing three SSAO techniques of differing physical accuracy found SSAO makes visually complex synthetic images appear more realistic, and that as scenes get more complex, lower physical accuracy is often sufficient — in a complex, ecologically valid scenario participants could not distinguish two SSAO methods despite clear technical quality differences. AO 'mainly contributes to spatial clarity by reinforcing contact areas' and is described as supporting perceived rather than physical realism.
  - *Implication:* Permission to be cheap. A 32-64 ray per-vertex AO bake at CAGE resolution (581 verts) is enough; you do not need a high-quality integrator. AO is what will finally give the nasolabial fold, the eye socket, the neck-under-jaw, the armpit and the crotch actual depth instead of the current uniform ambient=0.42 wash.
  - Source: https://projekter.aau.dk/the-perceptual-influence-of-approximating-ambient-occlusion-in-real-time-rendering-3a81e4fb.html
- **In a VR study ranking lighting features, participants rated Global Illumination most important, closely followed by Direct Shadows, with Ambient Occlusion and Reflections LESS prioritized.**
  - Study on the role of different light settings on perception of realism in virtual replicas in immersive VR: GI > direct shadows >> AO, reflections in participant-rated importance.
  - *Implication:* Sets the priority order for MiniMaster's rasterizer. A constant `ambient=0.42` is the worst possible GI approximation — it adds zero shape information in shadow. Replacing it with a hemispheric sky/ground ambient (and later a bent-normal-driven one) is a bigger perceptual win than adding specular, and it is ~3 lines. Direct shadows come second — a shadow term is what makes the chin sit on the neck and the arm sit on the ribcage.
  - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0097849323002583
- **Asymmetry and imperfection are deliberate, standard practice — sculptors intentionally drop symmetry at the secondary-form stage, and 'automatic asymmetry helps to overcome the uncanny valley'.**
  - Practitioner consensus: 'perfectly symmetrical faces can look fake, while small differences like one eyebrow being higher make faces look more realistic'; artists work 'without symmetry in order to give more realism to the model' when handling secondary forms; the recommended Blender workflow is to introduce subtle asymmetry (tilted smile, raised eyebrow) AFTER primary forms are locked.
  - *Implication:* MiniMaster's cage is mirror-symmetric by construction and every slider applies symmetrically (see generator.py's socket_l/socket_r pairs driven by the same value). A single `asymmetry` slider (0-1) plus a per-character random seed that injects small independent L/R deltas is a ~40-line change that is arguably the highest realism-per-line-of-code item in this entire report, and it costs nothing at render time.
  - Source: https://yelzkizi.org/realistic-character-creation-process-in-blender/
- **Shape and reflectance carry different information, and neither substitutes for the other — reflectance matters more for some judgments than shape does.**
  - Study on contributions of shape and reflectance to social judgments from faces: shape was more predictive of dominance, extroversion and trustworthiness; but male faces' attractiveness relied MORE on reflectance than shape. Separately, perceived intrinsic 3D shape of faces is robust to changes in lighting direction, image rotation and polarity inversion — i.e. viewers extract shape reliably regardless of lighting.
  - *Implication:* Two consequences. (1) You cannot shade your way out of bad geometry — viewers read shape robustly under any lighting, so a bad jaw stays a bad jaw. (2) But you also cannot sculpt your way to realism with a single flat albedo, because reflectance carries independent identity-relevant signal. This is the empirical basis for splitting the week's work across BOTH the cage and the shader rather than either alone.
  - Source: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0042698919301944
- **MiniMaster shades in gamma space with no tone mapping — a correctness bug that independently causes the clay look.**
  - render.py converts hex to 0-1 (line 25), multiplies by the shade term (line 190), and writes straight to uint8 (line 191). There is no linearization of the base color and no encode-to-sRGB after shading. Multiplying an sRGB-encoded albedo by a linear N·L produces a terminator that falls off too fast and midtones that are too dark, which is why the results look chalky/matte rather than fleshy.
  - *Implication:* Three lines: base_linear = base**2.2; do all shading in linear; out = clip(linear,0,1)**(1/2.2). Combined with a Reinhard or filmic tone map (x/(1+x) or the ACES fit), this alone visibly changes the material read of the existing renderer before any SSS work. It is the cheapest item on the list and it must land FIRST, because every parameter you tune afterwards (wrap widths, specular intensity, AO strength) will be mis-tuned if you tune them against gamma-space math.
  - Source: https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-14-advanced-techniques-realistic-real-time-skin
- **Gouraud shading of the SHADE VALUE (MiniMaster's current design) structurally forbids specular, Fresnel rim, and pre-integrated SSS.**
  - render.py:150-152 computes `corner_shade` per triangle corner and interpolates the scalar per pixel (line 189). Specular lobes are high-frequency: interpolating a specular value across a triangle makes highlights wobble, band, and vanish between vertices. Every technique in this report (Kelemen/Szirmay-Kalos specular, Schlick Fresnel rim, curvature-parameterized SSS, per-pixel noise breakup) requires the NORMAL at the pixel, not the shade at the pixel.
  - *Implication:* This is the one architectural change gating everything else. In the existing inner loop you already compute barycentrics l0/l1/l2 over a bbox tile; interpolating a (3,) normal instead of a scalar and normalizing is ~6 extra lines and roughly 3x the arithmetic in the tile — negligible against the Python-level per-triangle loop that already dominates. Do this in the same commit as the linear-space fix.
  - Source: https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-14-advanced-techniques-realistic-real-time-skin
- **Hair, eyelids and lash-line are realism GATES, not polish — and MiniMaster has none of them.**
  - Artist accounts of the eye region specifically name the soft sclera/lid transition as coming partly from AO and lid shadow and partly from reflection of the upper eyelashes; MetaHuman ships a dedicated occlusion mesh purely to simulate eyelid shadowing. Hair grooming is treated as a mandatory stage of every realistic-character pipeline surveyed, not an optional one.
  - *Implication:* A bald, lidless, lashless head cannot exit the valley no matter how good the skin shader is — and by the consistency finding above, a great skin shader on that head is actively counterproductive. For a subdivision-cage + STL pipeline this means: eyelids must be CAGE geometry (new rings), hair must be a separate closed shell of chunky forms (printable), and lashes should be a shading cue (a darkened band + a contact-shadow term) rather than geometry.
  - Source: http://www.chrisevans3d.com/pub_blog/lets-talk-eyes/

- The per-channel wrap values I give (0.50/0.28/0.18 for R/G/B) are engineering starting points I chose, NOT published constants. The verified facts underneath are: wrap lighting is (N·L + a)/(1 + a); a normalization term of (2 + a)/(2*(1 + a)) is reported; and red penetrates skin deeper than green/blue. Tune the three values against reference photos.
- The Barré-Brisebois/Bouchard translucency parameters (distortion 0.15, power 3.0, scale 2.0) are typical values from that technique's common implementations, given as a starting point. The formula structure is well attested (the John Austin URP article was the search hit); the specific constants are tuning, not citation.
- Eye anatomy figures (24 mm eyeball diameter, ~12 mm iris, ~7.8 mm corneal radius of curvature, ~2.2-2.6 mm corneal protrusion, upper lid covering the top 1-2 mm of iris, positive canthal tilt of 2-4 deg) are standard ophthalmic/artist reference values I am stating from knowledge, NOT from a source I retrieved this session. They are stable, well-documented numbers, but verify against an anatomy reference before baking them into slider ranges.
- The facial-asymmetry magnitudes I recommend (0.8-2.5 mm landmark deltas) are NOT sourced. A relevant study surfaced in search (PMC10354989, subjective evaluation of facial asymmetry with 3D simulated images) but its summary did not give me detection thresholds. If you want defensible numbers, that paper is the place to look for layperson vs orthodontist detection thresholds.
- The 'three-zone' face coloring rule (yellow forehead / red midface / cool jaw) is long-standing portrait-painting and texturing convention, not something I verified in this session's results. My specific channel shifts (+8-12% R etc.) are invented starting points.
- No source I found quantifies the RELATIVE contribution of geometry vs shading vs texture as a single ratio, and I doubt a clean one exists. The closest evidence is directional: perceived 3D face shape is robust to lighting/rotation/polarity changes (so geometry errors survive any shader), while reflectance independently predicts some judgments better than shape does. The VR light-settings study gives an ordering WITHIN lighting only (GI > direct shadows > AO/reflections), which is what I used for shader priority.
- My performance estimates for the AO/thickness bake (581 verts x 64 rays vs 4,616 L1 triangles ~ 1.7e8 ray-tri tests; subdivision level vert/face counts 2312/9236/36932 at L1/L2/L3) are my own arithmetic from the stated cage counts (403+178 verts, 401+176 quads) plus the Catmull-Clark V+E+F rule. They are not measured. Prototype and time it before committing to a bake resolution.
- Print-resolution claims (resin ~35-50 um XY, keep features above ~0.15 mm at 28-32 mm scale) are from general knowledge, not verified this session. Worth a dedicated check if the print path matters as much as the render path.
- I did not get to research hair rendering or grooming in any depth — my budget ran out first. The hair recommendation is grounded only in the general observation that every realistic-character pipeline surveyed treats grooming as mandatory. Hair may well deserve its own research pass, especially the printable-chunky-forms vs render-only-strands split.

## Print-reality: what facial/anatomical geometry actually survives resin/FDM printing at 28-32mm vs display scales, with mm thresholds, and what that means for MiniMaster's cage, sliders and exporter.

### Findings

- **The practical design floor for MSLA/SLA is 0.2 mm minimum feature, even though the pixel is 25-75 µm.**
  - Mainstream MSLA pixel pitch is 25-75 µm (Formlabs Form 4 quoted at 50 µm, 405 nm). But SLA design guidelines recommend a 0.2 mm minimum printable feature, 0.5 mm minimum slot, 1.0 mm minimum reliable pin diameter, and tolerance ±0.15% with a ±0.02 mm floor for 1-30 mm features. The gap between 0.05 mm pixel and 0.2 mm design rule exists because sub-0.2 mm geometry under-cures and shears off during support removal / post-cure.
  - *Implication:* This is the single hard number the generator must respect. Any slider whose full deflection moves the surface less than ~0.2 mm at export scale produces geometry that literally does not exist on the print. See the next finding — that is currently ALL 22 facial sliders.
  - Source: https://xometry.pro/en-uk/articles/3d-printing-sla-design-tips/
- **At a 32 mm export, every one of MiniMaster's 22 facial sliders moves the surface LESS than the 0.2 mm print floor at full deflection.**
  - generator.py:130 sets HEAD_UNIT = 0.138, so at 32 mm total height the head is Hh = 4.42 mm. The facial deltas are authored as fractions of Hh: eye_spacing 0.040·Hh = 0.177 mm, eye_height 0.030·Hh = 0.133 mm, eye_depth 0.028·Hh = 0.124 mm (generator.py:396-398), ear_size 0.035·Hh = 0.155 mm (generator.py:433), ear bump 0.012·Hh = 0.053 mm (generator.py:436). Eyeball radius is 0.056·Hh = 0.248 mm (Ø0.50 mm) at generator.py:449, and it sits recessed in a socket.
  - *Implication:* The deep-customization promise is currently unprintable at the stated output target. Full-left vs full-right on eye_depth differs by 0.12 mm — below the 0.2 mm floor, roughly 2 printer pixels, and invisible under primer. The fix is not more sliders; it is re-expressing slider magnitudes in absolute mm at the export scale.
  - Source: https://xometry.pro/en-uk/articles/3d-printing-sla-design-tips/
- **Scale arithmetic: a 32 mm figure is ~1:56, which puts most real facial features below or at the print floor.**
  - 1.8 m human at 32 mm total = 1:56.25. Derived printed sizes: head height 230 mm→4.1 mm; eye opening width 30 mm→0.53 mm; eye aperture height ~10 mm→0.18 mm; iris 12 mm→0.21 mm; pupil 4 mm→0.07 mm; nose length 50 mm→0.89 mm; alar width 35 mm→0.62 mm; nostril 8 mm→0.14 mm; upper-lip vermilion 8 mm→0.14 mm; philtrum depth 1.5 mm→0.027 mm; nasolabial fold depth 2 mm→0.036 mm; zygomatic projection 9 mm→0.16 mm; brow-ridge projection 5 mm→0.09 mm; ear height 60 mm→1.07 mm; helix thickness 3 mm→0.05 mm; finger diameter 18 mm→0.32 mm; inter-finger gap 3 mm→0.05 mm. (My arithmetic from standard anthropometry, not a cited table.)
  - *Implication:* Gives a hard survive/die list. SURVIVES at 32 mm: skull mass, jaw width, chin projection, nose as a whole, lip mass as one wedge, ear as a flat blob, cheekbone/brow only if exaggerated ~2x. DIES at 32 mm: eyelids, nostril holes, philtrum, nasolabial fold, vermilion border, iris/pupil, separate fingers, ear helix/tragus, wrinkles, hair strands. MiniMaster's existing omissions (no eyelids, no nostrils, no fingers, no hair) are CORRECT for 32 mm — the error is in the slider amplitudes, not the topology.
  - Source: https://ameralabs.com/blog/miniature-scale-3d-printing/
- **Commercial miniature sculptors solve the small-face problem with heroic exaggeration, quantified at roughly head 1.3x, hands 1.5x.**
  - Heroic scale deliberately enlarges heads (~1.3x), hands (~1.5x), feet, weapons and facial features for readability at arm's length across a table. Sourced numbers: at 32 mm tall, a realistic head is 4 mm while a heroic head is 6 mm; a 7-heads-tall figure with a 5 mm head stands 35 mm. Other summaries put heads 5-15% over anatomical ratio, so 1.3x is the aggressive end of a 1.05-1.5x range. A realistically-proportioned 32 mm figure is only ~5.5 heads tall.
  - *Implication:* HEAD_UNIT = 0.138 (1/7.25 heads) is realistic-canon, which means at the 32 mm 'medium' preset MiniMaster produces exactly the 4 mm head the sources describe as reading 'tiny' next to commercial minis. A print-scale-driven exaggeration layer is needed, not a change to the anatomy defaults.
  - Source: https://zandoriastudios.com/2019/07/10/the-problem-of-miniature-scales/
- **MiniMaster's SIZE_PRESETS use total height, but tabletop '28mm/32mm scale' is conventionally sole-to-eye — so every export is ~7% undersized.**
  - export.py:19 sets SIZE_PRESETS = {tiny 15, small 24, medium 32, large 45, huge 60} and export.py:68 scales total Z extent to that value. But the convention in the sources is that a 28 mm scale model measures 28 mm from soles to EYES. Eye height is ~0.93-0.94 of stature, so a nominal 32 mm-to-eye figure is ~34.2 mm total.
  - *Implication:* A one-line-ish fix with real consequences: MiniMaster minis are currently ~7% short next to Hero Forge / Eldritch Foundry output at the same nominal scale, and every derived mm threshold in the DFM work shifts with it.
  - Source: https://alkony.enerla.net/english/the-nexus/miniatures-nexus/miniature-hobby/miniature-size-miniature-scale
- **Structural extremities need 1.5 mm minimum, an order of magnitude above the 0.2 mm surface-relief floor — and real wrists/ankles at 32 mm fall below it.**
  - For structural elements — swords, limbs, staffs — a 1.5 mm minimum thickness is recommended; thinner becomes extremely brittle after post-curing. Sources also advise thickening extremities and DEEPENING recessed textures during design so detail survives cure. My arithmetic at 1:56: wrist (50 mm real) = 0.89 mm, ankle (60 mm) = 1.07 mm, neck (110 mm) = 1.96 mm.
  - *Implication:* Two distinct thresholds must be enforced separately: ~0.2 mm for surface relief amplitude, ~1.5 mm for load-bearing cross-sections (1.2 mm tolerable for a supported standing pose, 1.5 mm for anything cantilevered like an outstretched arm). This is exactly why heroic minis have thick wrists and ankles — it is a print constraint, not a style choice.
  - Source: https://3dfilamentinsider.com/how-to-design-miniatures-for-3d-printing/
- **Eyes at 28-32 mm are handled as a single almond socket recess plus paint, never as eyelid geometry.**
  - Standard practice: paint the entire eye socket black, lay a thin horizontal white line, then dot a tiny black pupil, leaving black visible above and below the white to IMPLY the eyelid lines. Painters use a Sakura Pigma Micron 005 for regular 28 mm humanoids. The sculpted geometry supplies a recess; the eyelids are painted illusion.
  - *Implication:* Confirms the target: at 32 mm the cage should carve one almond aperture per eye roughly 0.7 mm wide x 0.4 mm tall (exaggerated ~1.3x wide, ~2.2x tall over the anatomical 0.53 x 0.18 mm) so a brush can land inside it. MiniMaster's Ø0.50 mm recessed eyeball is both too small and hidden — the sphere should sit PROUD of the socket floor so the union leaves a convex, paintable bump.
  - Source: https://warhammerguild.com/painting/how-to-paint-eyes-miniatures/
- **50 µm layer height is the tested optimum for 28-35 mm minis; 20 µm only for ≤15 mm; going to 10 µm triples print time for marginal gain.**
  - AmeraLabs tested 100/80/50/20/10 µm on one plate (Elegoo Mars 4 DLP, TGM-7 resin, 28 mm Flamehorn Wyrmling + Maznar Column). Result: 50 µm gives sharp scales and smooth bases in one third the time of 10 µm; 20 µm is a marginal improvement; 20 µm recommended for 15 mm or smaller; 80-100 µm for vehicles/terrain.
  - *Implication:* Sets the mesh-density budget. With a 50 µm layer and ~50 µm pixel, geometry finer than ~0.05 mm chord deviation is wasted bytes. My computed facet edges: head shell at subdiv L3 = 176x4^3 = 11,264 quads over a ~40 mm² head → ~0.06 mm edges (right at the pixel, correct). Body at L3 = 401x64 = 25,664 quads over ~570 mm² → ~0.15 mm edges (~3 pixels, marginal). L4 body = 102,656 quads → ~0.075 mm. So: L3 for 28-40 mm, L4 only for ≥75 mm.
  - Source: https://ameralabs.com/blog/layer-height-for-miniatures/
- **Orientation for a standing figure: lean back 10-15°, face pointing up, supports on back of head/neck/shoulders and the base.**
  - For humanoid models, miniatures and figurines the optimal tilt is 10-15° from the Y axis; a slight BACKWARD lean of 10-15° keeps supports off the face while staying stable. The rules quoted: never place a support on the face; orient so the face points upward away from the build plate; concentrate supports on the back, the base, or the underside. Orientation is described as accounting for '90% of success', mainly by cutting suction forces.
  - *Implication:* Two generator consequences. (1) The exporter should offer auto-orient: rotate 10-15° about X (backward lean) and report the protected zone. (2) Since the face must be an UP-facing surface, deeply overhanging brow shelves and under-chin/under-nose geometry become downward-facing islands needing supports — so at 32 mm the brow should be a smooth ramp rather than a shelf. (Point 2 is my inference from the orientation rules, not directly stated.)
  - Source: https://dreaming3d.net/blogs/news/the-complete-guide-to-resin-print-orientation-master-the-45-degree-rule-and-when-to-break-it
- **Hollowing is for display scale only — a 28-32 mm figure should print solid.**
  - Recommended resin wall thickness is 1.2-2.5 mm (small models 1.2 mm+, medium/large 2 mm+, most people use 2-3 mm), with 2+ drain holes of 2-4 mm diameter, ≥3 mm typical, and ≥3.5 mm for fully enclosed cavities to prevent trapped resin and suction cups. My arithmetic: a 32 mm human is ~1:56, so ~7e7 mm³ / 56.25³ ≈ 390 mm³ ≈ 0.4 ml of resin, in a torso only ~8 mm thick — a 2 mm wall would leave a ~4 mm cavity that cannot take a 3.5 mm drain hole.
  - *Implication:* Gates the feature by size: solid below ~60 mm; above that (75 mm figures, 120 mm busts) hollow at 2 mm walls with two ≥3 mm drain holes. export.py already computes volume_mm3 at line 185, so the gate is nearly free.
  - Source: https://3dprinterly.com/best-wall-thickness-for-resin-3d-prints-miniatures-more/
- **Realistic facial geometry starts paying off around a 75 mm figure and is fully worthwhile at bust scale (head height ≥ ~19 mm).**
  - My arithmetic. A 75 mm figure is ~1:23: eye opening 1.3 mm, iris 0.5 mm, nostril 0.35 mm, vermilion 0.35-0.43 mm, zygomatic projection 0.39 mm, helix thickness 0.13 mm, finger diameter 0.78 mm, inter-finger gap 0.13 mm, a 1 cm hair lock 0.43 mm. Almost everything clears the 0.2 mm floor except the subtlest folds. At a 1:10 bust (head ~23 mm): eye opening 3 mm, iris 1.2 mm, nostril 0.8 mm, nasolabial fold 0.2 mm, philtrum 0.15 mm — the full MetaHuman-style slider set becomes real geometry. Bust/display scales cited in the sources: 75 mm = 1/24, 120 mm busts in the 1/10-ish display range, 200 mm = 1/9, 180 mm = 1/10.
  - *Implication:* Defines the scale gates for feature investment: eyelid loops and nostril geometry unlock at head height ≥ ~10 mm (≈75 mm figure); separate fingers at ≥ ~54 mm figure and safely at 75 mm with widened gaps; individual hair locks at ≥75 mm; skin-level detail never (pores at 1:10 are 0.02 mm). It also says the DEEP-customization goal is a display-scale product, and 32 mm is the degraded LOD of it.
  - Source: https://www.planetfigure.com/threads/bust-scales.21558/
- **Hero Forge / Eldritch Foundry — the named comparables — ship at 28-32 mm printed at 12K / 20 µm layers and market face-level customization down to teeth and eyebrows.**
  - Print services for these platforms quote 28-32 mm miniatures printed at 12K with 0.02 mm layers; Hero Forge advertises customizing the face down to teeth and eyebrows with a deeper face customizer planned; Eldritch Foundry sells STL downloads at $5.99 and resin prints from $25. Customer reports cite visible coat buttons on ~1.5 inch (38 mm) figures.
  - *Implication:* Sets the competitive bar and validates the approach: they use the finest available layer height (20 µm, the AmeraLabs 'marginal gain' tier) precisely because faces at this scale need every micron. A coat button is ~15 mm real → 0.27 mm at 1:56, i.e. just above the 0.2 mm floor — consistent with my threshold math and a good calibration target for 'smallest thing that reads'.
  - Source: https://www.etsy.com/listing/1077884518/custom-12k-hero-forge-eldritch-foundry
- **FDM cannot do a 32 mm face; a 0.2 mm nozzle is the floor and resin is still the right tool.**
  - A standard 0.4 mm nozzle at 0.2 mm layers is described as physically incapable of resolving 28 mm-scale facial expressions, chainmail or thin weapons. The workable FDM setup is a 0.2 mm nozzle, 0.08-0.1 mm layers, 25-30 mm/s, minimum feature = nozzle diameter (0.2 mm), taking 3-6 h per 28 mm mini vs 45 min standard — and sources still say resin is the better tool for 10 mm faces and sub-1 mm textures.
  - *Implication:* FDM's 0.2 mm minimum feature happens to equal the MSLA design floor, so one DFM threshold covers both — but FDM's 0.08-0.1 mm layers (2x coarser than the 50 µm resin optimum) mean the vertical/stair-step budget differs. Practical output: emit the same STL, but the validator should take a target-process flag and warn that facial sliders are meaningless on FDM below ~50 mm figures.
  - Source: https://3dprintbook.com/blog/tuning-fdm-printers-for-miniatures/
- **Commercial print-service DFM rules are far more conservative than hobby MSLA and a 32 mm mini cannot meet them at all.**
  - MyMiniFactory's grey resin service specifies minimum detail thickness 2 mm, minimum wall thickness 3 mm, one or two drain holes ≥3.5 mm diameter, closed/oriented/single mesh, STL or OBJ under 100 MB, bed 266 x 145 x 390 mm.
  - *Implication:* Two things. (1) If MiniMaster ever targets a service bureau rather than a home printer, the whole DFM envelope shifts by 10x and only display-scale output qualifies. (2) The 100 MB cap and 'closed, oriented, single mesh' requirement are worth encoding in the exporter — MiniMaster already checks watertight/outward (export.py:87-88, parts.py:75) but ships head, body and two eyeballs as separate shells (generator.py:534-538), which fails 'single mesh' unless fused.
  - Source: https://creator.myminifactory.com/migrated/3d-models-optimization-for-grey-resin-prints

### Recommendations

- [medium/transformative] **Re-express every facial slider delta in absolute mm at export scale instead of fractions of HEAD_UNIT, with a documented reference scale and a per-scale amplification curve.** — Author all 22 facial sliders so full deflection = ±0.5 to ±1.5 mm at a 75 mm reference figure (head height 10.4 mm), then multiply by an exaggeration factor E(s) when exporting smaller. Currently eye_spacing/eye_height/eye_depth resolve to 0.177/0.133/0.124 mm at 32 mm (generator.py:396-398) — all under the 0.2 mm floor. Target at 32 mm after amplification: eye_spacing ±0.35 mm, eye_depth ±0.30 mm, cheek_depth ±0.30 mm, nose_bridge_height ±0.35 mm, brow_depth ±0.30 mm. Ramp E linearly in log(figure_height) from E=2.4 at 32 mm to E=1.0 at 75 mm and above.
- [medium/high] **Add a numpy DFM validator that reports, in mm at export scale, the minimum local thickness and the minimum surface-relief amplitude, and fails/warns per zone.** — For each cage vertex cast a ray inward along -normal to the opposite surface to get a local diameter. Thresholds: 1.5 mm for cantilevered extremities (outstretched arm, weapon, staff), 1.2 mm for supported limbs, 0.8 mm for wrists/ankles in a standing pose, 0.2 mm for surface relief peak-to-valley. My arithmetic says wrists land at 0.89 mm and ankles at 1.07 mm at 32 mm, so the validator will fire immediately and correctly. Report as a table alongside the existing volume_mm3/size_mm block in export.py:184-188.
- [medium/transformative] **Add a print-scale-driven heroic exaggeration layer applied at export, separate from the anatomy sliders.** — At 32 mm apply: head x1.30 (HEAD_UNIT 0.138 → 0.179, giving a 5.7 mm head at 32 mm total, matching the sourced 6 mm heroic head vs 4 mm realistic), hands x1.5, feet x1.25, wrist/ankle/neck minimum-diameter clamp to 1.2 mm, nose projection x1.4, eye socket aperture x2.0 vertically. Ramp all factors to 1.0 by 75 mm. Keep the sliders anatomically honest so the same character reads as realistic at bust scale and as a readable mini at 32 mm.
- [small/high] **Fix the scale convention: make SIZE_PRESETS sole-to-eye with an explicit scale_mode.** — export.py:19 currently scales TOTAL Z extent to {tiny 15, small 24, medium 32, large 45, huge 60}. Tabletop convention measures soles to EYES. Add scale_mode='eye' (default) and divide by the eye-height ratio 0.935, so 'medium' 32 mm-to-eye = 34.2 mm total, 'small' 24 → 25.7, 'large' 45 → 48.1. Compute eye height from the actual eye-line ring rather than the constant where possible.
- [medium/high] **Rebuild the eye treatment as a scale-gated pair: almond socket recess below 75 mm, real lid loops above.** — At 32 mm carve ONE almond aperture per eye, 0.70 mm wide x 0.40 mm tall x 0.15 mm deep (vs the anatomical 0.53 x 0.18 mm — deliberately widened so a Micron 005 / fine brush tip can land inside), and raise the eyeball radius from 0.056·Hh (Ø0.50 mm, generator.py:449) so the sphere sits 0.05-0.10 mm PROUD of the socket floor, guaranteeing a convex bump survives the union. Only above head height 10 mm (≈75 mm figure) add the upper/lower lid loops — that is where the 0.35 mm lid margin clears the 0.2 mm floor.
- [medium/high] **Add a 'print preview' render mode: clay-grey matcap with strong ambient occlusion, plus a relief-resolution overlay that paints any surface below the 0.2 mm amplitude threshold in red.** — The current rasterizer is flat/gouraud with a single diffuse color and no AO, which makes it impossible to judge whether a fold will read. Compute per-vertex relief amplitude as the deviation from a low-pass (2-3 Catmull-Clark levels coarser) version of the same surface, convert to mm at export scale, and color-code: red < 0.2 mm, amber 0.2-0.3 mm, neutral above. This turns the whole finding set into live feedback while sliders move.
- [small/medium] **Auto-select subdivision level from export height and stop shipping wasted triangles.** — L2 for ≤20 mm, L3 for 28-45 mm, L4 for ≥75 mm. My computed facet edges vs the 50 µm pixel / 50 µm layer optimum: head at L3 = 11,264 quads over ~40 mm² → 0.06 mm edges (correct); body at L3 = 25,664 quads over ~570 mm² → 0.15 mm edges (marginal, ~3 px); body at L4 = 102,656 quads → 0.075 mm. Whole figure at L4 ≈ 296k triangles ≈ 15 MB binary STL, comfortably under MyMiniFactory's 100 MB cap.
- [medium/medium] **Add auto-orientation and a hollowing gate to the exporter.** — Auto-orient: rotate the figure 10-15° backward about X so the face is up-facing, and emit the 'protected zone' (face, chest front) in the report so a slicer operator knows where not to support. Hollowing: gate on figure height ≥60 mm, then 2.0 mm walls with two ≥3.0 mm drain holes placed on the underside of the base or the back of the head; below 60 mm print solid (a 32 mm human is only ~390 mm³ ≈ 0.4 ml of resin, so hollowing saves nothing and invites suction failure).
- [large/medium] **Keep mittens at 32 mm — but scale-gate fingers, hair locks and ear cartilage rather than omitting them permanently.** — Fingers: unlock at ≥54 mm figure (0.55 mm diameter), safe at 75 mm (0.78 mm diameter, 0.13 mm anatomical gap — widen modeled gaps to 0.30 mm minimum so they don't fuse). Hair: never strands (a real 0.07 mm strand is 0.001 mm at 1:56); model as masses/ribbons with ≥0.4 mm thickness and ≥0.3 mm grooves at 32 mm; individual locks unlock at 75 mm (a 1 cm lock = 0.43 mm). Ears: keep the vertex bump at 32 mm (helix thickness is 0.05 mm, unprintable); add helix/tragus loops only at ≥75 mm (0.13 mm — still needs ~2x thickening).
- [small/medium] **Fuse head, body and eyeballs into a single mesh at export and record the fused triangle count and file size.** — generator.py:534-538 emits four separate shells (body, head, eye_l, eye_r). Service-bureau rules require a closed, oriented, SINGLE mesh under 100 MB. export.py already has fuse plumbing and watertight checks (export.py:87-88, 154-158, parts.py:75) — make single-mesh output the default for STL and surface the fused triangle count in the report.

### Uncertainties

- Research was cut short: the session hit its 200-call WebSearch budget after 8 query rounds, and WebFetch returned HTTP 403 on every host I tried (zandoriastudios.com, battlehonours3d.com, 3dprinterly.com, formlabs.com, ameralabs.com). All source content below comes from search-result summaries, not from reading the pages directly. Numbers attributed to Zandoria Studios (4 mm realistic vs 6 mm heroic head at 32 mm; 7-heads figure with 5 mm head = 35 mm) were reported in a search summary but I could NOT open the page to verify them — treat as likely-correct but unconfirmed.
- All per-feature mm conversions (eye opening 0.53 mm, nostril 0.14 mm, nasolabial fold 0.036 mm, finger diameter 0.32 mm, etc.) are MY arithmetic from standard anthropometric dimensions divided by the scale factor. They are not quoted from any source. The scale factor itself and the underlying real-world dimensions (eye opening 30 mm, iris 12 mm, nose length 50 mm, philtrum depth 1.5 mm) are conventional figures I did not verify against a cited anthropometric table.
- The eye-height-to-stature ratio of 0.935 used for the sole-to-eye scale fix is a commonly cited anthropometric ratio that I did not verify in this research. If the builder wants the scale convention exactly right, this specific number should be confirmed before changing SIZE_PRESETS.
- I could not find a source giving the minimum RELIEF AMPLITUDE (as opposed to minimum wall/feature thickness) that a miniature sculptor considers visible after priming and painting. The 0.2 mm floor I use throughout is the SLA minimum-feature design guideline, which is about whether geometry survives curing, not about whether a human eye can see it under paint. The true 'visible after primer' threshold is plausibly higher — perhaps 0.3-0.4 mm — but I have no citation and did not verify primer/paint layer thicknesses. My recommendations use 0.2 mm as the hard fail and 0.3 mm as an amber warning; both should be validated with a physical test print.
- I could not run a dedicated search on undercut avoidance or on separate-fingers minimum diameter (budget exhausted). The undercut/overhang guidance in Finding 9 (brow shelves and under-chin geometry becoming supported islands) is my inference from the orientation rules, not a sourced claim. Note also that undercut avoidance matters far more for silicone-mold casting than for direct resin printing, where the dominant failure modes are suction cups and unsupported islands — I did not research the casting path at all.
- Commercial sculptors' actual working practice at 32 mm (how much they exaggerate cheekbones specifically, what relief depth they carve for a nasolabial fold) was not found in any source. The exaggeration multipliers I recommend (nose x1.4, eye aperture x2.0 vertical, cheek relief to 0.30 mm) are derived from the printability thresholds, not from documented sculptor practice.
- The Hero Forge / Eldritch Foundry detail claims come from an Etsy print-service listing and marketing copy, not from technical documentation. '12K at 0.02 mm' is a print-service spec, and I found nothing about their actual mesh density, slider implementation, or whether they apply scale-dependent exaggeration.
- Facet-edge-length and STL-size figures (0.06 mm head edges at L3, 0.15 mm body edges at L3, ~296k triangles / ~15 MB at L4) are my computations from the cage counts in the code combined with estimated surface areas (head ~40 mm², body ~570 mm²). The surface-area estimates are rough; the conclusion (L3 adequate at 32 mm, L4 for 75 mm+) is robust to a fair amount of error in them, but the exact numbers are not.
