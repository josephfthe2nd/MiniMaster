# Appendage references: sources for anatomically correct junctions

Status: reference list. It records **where** the anatomical references for tails, horns, wing roots, extra limbs and the skin transition came from, what each shows and the key numbers read from them. It contains **no images and no copied text**: every entry is a title, creator, licence, page URL and a one-line description written for this project.

Why it exists: appendages that read as "slapped over the skin" lack the bone, muscle and skin layering and the skin transition real bodies have (see [junctions.md](junctions.md) for the mechanism that moves the body skin around an appendage root, [anatomy.md](anatomy.md) and [creature_anatomy.md](creature_anatomy.md)). Engineers use this list to build junctions from real proportions; reviewers use it to compare a render with a real reference of the same view.

## How to read it

- Ids are `region/id`; the region names the collection pass (tail, horn, wing, limb, skin, general). The horn and wing passes both use A01.. and H01.., so always include the region.
- Licence is the normalised short form of what the source page said when it was collected (public domain `PD`, `CC0`, `CC-BY-x`, `CC-BY-SA-x`, one `GFDL-1.2`). No NonCommercial or NoDerivatives material is listed. **Re-check the licence on the page URL before ever redistributing an image**; attribution (creator + licence + page) is required for the CC-BY and CC-BY-SA entries and share-alike applies to the CC-BY-SA ones.
- Images are kept outside the repository. Internal review copies only; do not commit them.
- Left out because their licence status is unclear: one historical medical photograph of a human caudal appendage (listed as Commons CC-BY-4.0 but the holding collection now says in copyright) and one modern commercial figurine photograph.
- 328 unique images are listed (357 entries were collected; 27 were the same picture filed under a second region and are noted as "also" on the first entry).

## Key numbers

Each value was read off one image by hand-placed pixel landmarks (error 5-10 % on flat plates and scale-bar photos, 10-20 % on perspective photos, up to 30 % on stylised prints). Treat them as proportions that bracket a design, not as survey data. Ids refer to the source tables below. Ratios are in the unit named; r is the base radius of the protrusion.
